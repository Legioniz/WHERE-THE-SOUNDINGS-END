# Act III — The Deep

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-12

## Dramatic purpose

Connect the present incidents with the older maritime history and reconstruct what the Orison crew attempted to do. This act should deliver the strongest emotional material while refusing to turn the Choir into a fully explained villain.

**Dramatic question:** What did the Orison crew know, and why did they choose to divert their survey route?

## Region focus

- **The Drowned Parish:** submerged settlement remains, records, bell history and evidence of earlier incidents.
- **The Hollow Trench:** deep-water survey approaches and the strongest recurring signal pattern.

The Drowned Parish should feel like a place with human history, not merely an underwater dungeon. The Hollow Trench should be approached through progressively more difficult survey decisions, not an abrupt teleport to the finale.

## Sequence

### 1. The Drowned Parish record

The player recovers a historical record connected to the old bell and an earlier sounding incident. The record predates the Orison and gives a second independent source for the same repeated-return motif.

The Parish's evidence should distinguish:
- what the recorder observed;
- what the recorder believed;
- what was later copied into local folklore;
- what cannot be verified.

The player learns that the bell's silence was a deliberate response to an earlier event or a practical decision made after an unexplained incident. The precise cause remains open until the evidence chain is complete.

### 2. The Orison's course

The player finds a chart fragment, survey log or equivalent evidence showing the Orison changed course away from Greyhaven. The captain did not simply pursue the signal. The route was altered to reduce the likelihood that repeated transmissions would draw the event toward the harbour.

This discovery complicates the protagonist's memory. The father was not concealing a simple rescue route; he was trying to protect people who were not on the vessel.

### 3. Nell's role

Separate recordings and crew notes show that Nell remained on the radio after the crew began limiting transmissions. She used a controlled signal to keep a record of the route and warn a future listener.

The protagonist's private call appears in the record, but the later evidence shows that hearing the pattern does not establish that Nell was alive at the time of reception. The player should be able to understand the difference between the original message and the later repeated signal.

### 4. The crew's decision

The best-supported reconstruction is that the crew recognised the danger of following or answering the signal. The captain ordered a diversion. Nell maintained a final transmission to preserve a navigational warning. The crew was lost despite those actions.

Do not make the captain's decision perfectly correct or reveal a complete mechanical explanation for the loss. Preserve room for uncertainty about the final minutes.

## Act III reversal

The protagonist discovers that the message they spent eight years treating as a plea was, in context, a warning. Nell was not asking them to return to the sound. She was asking a future listener to record the position and stop following it.

The emotional conflict shifts from "How do I bring them home?" to "How do I prevent another crew from being lost?"

## Act III required outcomes

- Player has found one historical record from before the Orison.
- Player has evidence of the Orison's route diversion.
- Player has at least two independent pieces of evidence about Nell's radio actions.
- Player can explain why a familiar voice is not proof of a living speaker.
- Player has a practical lead to the final sounding area.

## Optional content

- Additional crew notes showing disagreements about the course.
- A damaged instrument part that helps identify a false reading.
- A record of an earlier vessel that chose not to answer the call and survived.
- A non-hostile deep-sea ecological site that reminds the player the deep is not inherently evil.

## Exit condition

The player has enough evidence to interpret Nell's final warning and reaches the final deep-water survey boundary.

## Implementation status

Proposed narrative content only. Deep-ocean survey profiles and authored site markers exist as technical foundations; the story sites, crew records, quest chain and cutscenes described here remain unimplemented.
