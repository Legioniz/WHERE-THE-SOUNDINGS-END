# Main Story — The Choir and the Last Mark

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.3  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-13

## 1. Narrative promise

A working mariner returns to an isolated island community and takes a job on an old fishing boat. Three boats have gone beyond the outer marker; only one has returned. The survivor asks whether the lighthouse bell is still ringing, even though the bell has been silent for eleven years.

The protagonist knows something about impossible soundings. Eight years earlier, they were the apprentice sounder aboard the survey vessel Orison, which vanished near the Hollow Trench. Their older sister Nell was the radio operator. Their father was the captain. The protagonist was the only person recovered.

As the player fishes, works, travels and surveys, familiar maritime sounds begin to contradict the instruments. A second sounding arrives after the first. A radio voice uses a private family call. A bell is heard from water where no bell can be. Old records suggest the phenomenon existed long before the Orison.

The central mystery is called **the Choir** by people comparing the incidents. The name describes a recurring pattern of acoustic anomalies; it does not establish a creature, intelligence, species or supernatural explanation. The protagonist eventually discovers that the Orison crew had been trying to keep the signal away from Greyhaven. Nell's final message was not a request to be rescued. It was a warning: **"Don't follow the sound. Mark the water."**

The story is ultimately about grief, guilt, responsibility, memory and the limits of certainty. The sea does not provide a clean answer simply because the protagonist needs one.

## 2. Narrative status and relationship to current content

This is a proposed narrative direction, not approved final canon and not an implemented campaign. It is designed to fit the current Greyhaven dialogue foundation:
- The Harbourmaster introduces the player as a new hand on an old boat.
- Three boats are missing beyond the outer marker; one returned.
- The returned skipper asks whether the lighthouse bell is still ringing.
- The bell has been silent for eleven years.
- A sounding buoy has washed in near the western mudflat, and the Harbourmaster asks the player to recover it without switching it on.
- Mara Venn, Tomas Vale and Inez Sarmiento provide distinct practical observations.

The proposal retains the existing six-region world: Greyhaven, The Shallows, Blackrock Reach, The Drowned Parish, The Outer Shoals and The Hollow Trench. It does not assert that all regions are already connected or playable.

## 3. The central mystery — the Choir

The Choir is the working name for a family of recurring acoustic events associated with particular waters and historical incidents. It is experienced through delayed soundings, impossible or unstable depth returns, repeated radio calls, familiar voice contours, false bell tones, altered bearings and sudden changes in the ambient soundscape.

### What the player can observe

- A sounding pulse returns more than once, with a delay or bearing that does not match the expected seabed.
- A repeated measurement changes even when the boat and instrument appear stable.
- A radio transmission contains a voice or phrase that resembles someone missing.
- A bell-like tone is heard despite the lighthouse bell being physically silent.
- Wildlife, engine noise, waves or ordinary harbour sounds may briefly disappear from the player's perception or mix.
- Old records describe related events before the Orison incident.

### What is not established

- Whether the events share one physical cause.
- Whether the water, geology, equipment, human transmission or an unknown phenomenon is responsible.
- Whether the Choir has awareness, intention or a body.
- Whether a voice is copied from a previous transmission, reconstructed from acoustic information or misidentified by the listener.
- Whether Nell's final transmission was received live, delayed, repeated or reconstructed.

The game must not answer these questions with a single explanatory cutscene. Evidence can rule out specific interpretations without explaining everything.

### Rules for consistency

1. **Signals have context.** Important events occur in authored regions, conditions or investigative situations, not as arbitrary voice scares anywhere on the map.
2. **Repetition matters.** A first signal may be ordinary or ambiguous; the pattern becomes meaningful when independently verified.
3. **A voice is not proof of a living speaker.** The player must be able to separate a recording, an echo, an imitation and a person's current presence.
4. **Evidence is physical as well as acoustic.** Logs, damaged equipment, changed routes, missing objects, survey records and environmental observations support the story.
5. **Silence is a valid event.** The absence of waves, birds, engine harmonics or radio noise should be used sparingly and with deliberate pacing.
6. **The Choir is not every monster.** Legendary animals and regional ecology remain independent mysteries unless a specific story later establishes a connection.
7. **The phenomenon is not omnipotent.** It does not read minds, know every private fact or predict the future as a confirmed power.
8. **The player can test uncertainty.** Repeated pings, changed headings, visual landmarks, physical inspection and returning to harbour should help assess evidence.

## 4. The Orison truth

The Orison was conducting a survey near the deep-water route eight years before the game. The crew encountered repeated returns and increasingly convincing radio patterns. The captain concluded that continuing to answer the signals could draw the incident toward a populated harbour.

The captain ordered a course diversion and reduced unnecessary transmissions. The protagonist, then an apprentice, believed a call using Nell's private signal meant she was still reachable. That belief became the emotional centre of the incident and the source of their guilt.

The later evidence suggests that Nell remained at the radio to maintain a final transmission and to make the route of the dangerous signal legible to anyone who might investigate. Her message instructed the listener not to follow the voice but to mark the water: record the position, preserve the evidence, and do not confuse a signal with a person.

The Orison's exact final mechanical sequence, the fate of each crew member and Nell's physical fate remain unknown. The story should reveal the crew's intent, not provide a complete incident reconstruction.

## 5. Four-act campaign overview

| Act | Name | Primary region focus | Dramatic question | Main reversal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Familiar Waters | Greyhaven and The Shallows | What happened to the missing boats? | The first impossible return resembles the Orison incident |
| II | Beyond the Coast | Blackrock Reach and The Outer Shoals | Is the signal local, natural or following the vessels? | Historical evidence predates the Orison |
| III | The Deep | The Drowned Parish and approaches to The Hollow Trench | What did the Orison crew know? | The crew was trying to redirect the signal away from Greyhaven |
| IV | The Last Sounding | The Hollow Trench and Greyhaven aftermath | Will the protagonist follow the voice, silence the system or mark the truth? | Nell's final message is a warning, not a rescue request |

The acts are dramatic groupings, not a promise that every named region will be a fully built open-world level at launch. The vertical slice should prove one complete Greyhaven expedition first.

## 6. Mystery delivery

Deliver the story in overlapping layers:

- **Immediate layer:** missing boats, strange returns, bell warnings and harbour work.
- **Personal layer:** the Orison, Nell, the captain and the protagonist's memory.
- **Historical layer:** the silent bell, Drowned Parish records and earlier survey incidents.
- **Ethical layer:** the crew's decision to divert the signal away from a populated area.
- **Final layer:** the player chooses what to do with incomplete evidence and what account future mariners will inherit.

Do not let a single collectible or NPC conversation answer multiple layers at once. Important revelations should be supported by at least two different evidence forms.

## 7. Player agency and endings

The ending is shaped by evidence collected, interpretation established and the final action. There is no universal morality score.

- **The Answer:** the protagonist follows the familiar voice into the deepest signal and is lost.
- **The Silence:** the protagonist cuts the active chain of transmissions and survives, but leaves the central historical record incomplete.
- **The Last Mark:** the protagonist assembles the strongest evidence, records the location and names of the lost, then stops answering the signal. This is the recommended thematic ending.
- **The Keeper:** an optional secret epilogue concept in which the protagonist remains near the boundary as a warning presence. It is not required for the initial three-ending structure and should not be scoped before the main endings work.

See [Endings](ENDINGS.md) for eligibility, choice and aftermath requirements.

## 8. Themes and recurring motifs

| Motif | Literal use | Emotional use |
|---|---|---|
| Sounding pulse | Measures depth and reveals possible sites | The desire to know what is below |
| Delayed echo | Instrument response that arrives too late | Memory that returns changed |
| Bell | A navigational and communal warning | A promise the village no longer hears |
| Route mark | Chart notation and bearing record | Accepting that a location can be remembered without being reached |
| Quiet sea | Environmental state and audio event | The fear of losing the world that confirms one's presence |
| Old boat | The protagonist's livelihood | Returning to work without returning to the past |

## 9. Narrative production rules

- Let routine fishing and harbour life remain meaningful between horror beats.
- Use ordinary explanations first; let contradictions accumulate.
- Never place essential clues only in random events or time-limited NPC schedules.
- Ensure that a player who avoids optional horror encounters can still complete the main story.
- Let the journal distinguish direct observation, witness testimony, interpretation and unresolved questions.
- Avoid exposition dumps. A clue should change a route, a decision, an interpretation or a relationship.
- Do not use a jump scare as the sole delivery method for an important reveal.
- Do not make all missing people interchangeable. Their work, relationships and belongings should matter.
- Do not tie every sea legend to the Choir.

## 10. Implementation boundary

The current repository has a Yarn dialogue foundation and four Greyhaven residents. This proposed campaign is not yet integrated into quest logic, story-state persistence, final dialogue, voice, cutscenes or ending scenes.

Before implementation:
1. Approve this narrative baseline and the unresolved canon decisions.
2. Create a clue and quest data contract with stable IDs.
3. Map the first vertical-slice quest to existing Greyhaven interactions.
4. Add deterministic quest-state and evidence persistence through the existing save architecture.
5. Add regression coverage for clue acquisition, missed optional evidence and save/load.
6. Author Yarn content only after the state contract and dialogue ownership are agreed.

## Greyhaven evidence chain — Act I content expansion

The four Greyhaven residents now contribute independent, occupation-bounded records to the opening investigation. The Harbourmaster can show the bell's last-confirmed-sounding history; Mara records cold-centred catches from separate boats; Tomas describes hull scoring that does not match a single rock impact; and Inez preserves an empty, untorn net with its knots still fastened. A comparison topic becomes available as these records are discovered.

The synthesis is intentionally cautious: it identifies that several ordinary records remain unexplained, not that they prove a shared cause. Each resident retains their own voice and limits of knowledge. Core observations are not locked behind high trust; only more personal routines and hobbies use Familiar-or-higher relationship gates. This is source-authored Greyhaven dialogue, not a completed quest campaign or an approved final explanation of the Choir.
