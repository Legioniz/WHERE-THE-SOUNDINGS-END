# Act I — Familiar Waters

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-12

## Dramatic purpose

Make Greyhaven feel like a real working harbour before the player has reason to fear it. Teach the player to read the boat, the coastline, ordinary sounds and resident behaviour. Introduce the missing boats and the silent bell through testimony, not a cinematic explanation.

**Dramatic question:** What happened to the boats that went beyond the outer marker, and why does the returned skipper keep asking about a bell that has been silent for eleven years?

## Opening state

The protagonist arrives in Greyhaven after years away from deep-water survey work. They take a job as the new hand on an old boat. The Harbourmaster gives practical advice about the channel and warns that three boats travelled beyond the outer marker; only one returned.

The returned skipper is not available as an all-knowing exposition source. Their fragmented reaction is a clue to be investigated. The Harbourmaster's sounding-buoy recovery job provides an immediate practical task and a reason to use the Sounding Rig carefully.

## Sequence

### 1. Learn the harbour

- Establish the boat, its condition, basic controls and local navigation landmarks.
- Meet the Harbourmaster and understand the channel warnings.
- Visit Mara Venn, Tomas Vale and Inez Sarmiento in the course of ordinary work.
- Hear each resident's distinct observation: unusual catch condition, unexplained hull scoring/knocks, and nets that returned empty or damaged.
- Do not require the player to believe any supernatural explanation.

### 2. Recover the sounding buoy

- Accept the Harbourmaster's request to recover a buoy near the western mudflat.
- The explicit instruction is not to switch it on.
- Allow the player to retrieve it safely and inspect its exterior, cable or recorded condition.
- A later controlled test, performed after the player has a safe context and a reason to verify the device, can produce a delayed return.
- If the player does not activate it, preserve the clue through a recovered recording or controlled harbour test. Critical story progress must not be lost to a single caution choice.

### 3. First contradiction

A repeated sounding gives a depth or bearing response that should not occur under the current profile. The player can:
- repeat the pulse from a changed heading;
- compare the return with a landmark or chart;
- stop the boat and test whether movement or machinery noise explains it;
- record the result and return to harbour.

The second return should be unsettling but not immediately hostile. The player has earned the fear by verifying a contradiction.

### 4. The personal signal

A radio fragment contains a contour or phrase that resembles Nell's private call. It must be brief and incomplete. It does not say "come find me" or explain what happened. The protagonist reacts before the player has enough evidence to decide what the voice means.

This is the first point at which the Orison memory becomes difficult to suppress. Do not reveal the full incident history yet.

## Act I turning point

The player verifies that the returned pattern cannot be explained solely by a damaged buoy or ordinary shallow-water reflection. A separate source — a record, an independent sounding or a physical anomaly — confirms that the signal is not just a one-off equipment fault.

The Harbourmaster remains cautious. The residents disagree about what the evidence means. The player has a reason to travel farther, but no proof that a creature is responsible.

## Act I required outcomes

- Player understands the basic work loop and the importance of returning safely.
- Player knows three boats are missing and one returned.
- Player learns the lighthouse bell has been silent for eleven years.
- Player has encountered a repeatable or corroborated acoustic contradiction.
- Player has a personal reason to recognise the pattern, without learning the full Orison story.
- Player receives a clear lead toward a wider survey area.

## Optional content

- Additional market or repair conversations.
- Personal effects belonging to a missing crew.
- A normal marine sound that initially resembles the anomaly.
- A harmless wildlife sighting that reinforces that not every unusual event is hostile.
- A local account of the bell that may be mistaken or incomplete.

## Horror and safety constraints

- No mandatory early chase.
- No essential clue delivered only as an audio-only cue.
- The initial sounding anomaly should not damage the boat without warning.
- A player who follows the Harbourmaster's instruction and does not activate the buoy must still be able to progress.
- The safe harbour must remain a credible refuge.

## Exit condition

The player has one verified clue linking the present incidents to a wider acoustic pattern and has a clear destination or contact for Act II.

## Implementation status

Proposed narrative content only. Current Harbourmaster dialogue already contains the missing-boat warning, silent-bell question and sounding-buoy recovery job. The complete quest, buoy interaction, story state and Nell signal are not implemented by this document.
