# Character Arcs — The People of Greyhaven and the Orison

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed character-development baseline — not a completed quest or dialogue implementation  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-14

## 1. Purpose

This document develops the emotional movement of the core cast. It supplements the [Character Bible](../04_CHARACTERS_AND_ENTITIES/CHARACTER-BIBLE.md), [Protagonist](../04_CHARACTERS_AND_ENTITIES/PROTAGONIST.md) and current Yarn dialogue. It does not rename stable NPC IDs, create new save variables or claim that the proposed campaign is playable.

Characters should have a life before the player arrives and a life after a conversation ends. Their work, relationships, private habits and contradictions should be more visible than their ability to explain the mystery.

## 2. The Sounder — protagonist

**External role:** working mariner and former apprentice hydrographic sounder aboard the Orison.  
**Private pressure:** guilt about answering a familiar call before the vessel was lost.  
**Defensive habit:** turns emotion into a task; checks the equipment again rather than discussing what the result means.  
**False belief:** if the signal can be heard clearly enough, the past can be corrected.  
**Truth to learn:** a careful record can protect others even when it cannot restore the lost.

The Sounder arrives for work, not to solve a supernatural mystery. The opening observations reactivate memories that have never been reconciled. Each region supplies a different kind of evidence. The player first wants to know whether Nell survived, then wants a complete explanation of the Orison, and finally must decide what to do with evidence that cannot provide certainty.

The three proposed endings express different choices:
- **The Answer:** follow the voice and surrender to the need for certainty.
- **The Silence:** stop responding and protect the living, while leaving the record incomplete.
- **The Last Mark:** preserve the route, names and evidence, then refuse to follow the voice.

The protagonist's guilt must never be treated as proof that they caused the Orison loss. Their name, gender, age and fixed appearance remain undecided.

## 3. Nell — radio operator

**External role:** radio operator aboard the Orison and older sister to the Sounder.  
**Private pressure:** keep a usable record while the crew's situation deteriorates.  
**Defining traits:** technically competent, funny when tired, impatient with avoidable mistakes, familiar with her sibling's habits.  
**Risk:** later listeners mistake the private call pattern for proof that she is alive.

Nell is first encountered as a voice fragment, then as an operator's practical annotations, then as a person through ordinary correspondence and small habits, and finally as the author of a warning whose context has been misunderstood.

Do not write her as a constant pleading voice or an all-knowing guide. The player should discover what Nell could know at the time of each recording. Her actual fate remains unknown.

## 4. The Orison Captain — the Sounder's father

**External role:** master of the survey vessel.  
**Private pressure:** protect the crew and avoid carrying an unexplained hazard toward a populated harbour.  
**Defining traits:** decisive under pressure, not infallible, unwilling to present an uncertain route as safe.  
**Risk:** the Sounder interprets the course change as abandonment or a failed attempt to reach Nell.

Early records make the captain appear to have diverted away from the place where the crew might have been rescued. Later route and transmission evidence suggest the diversion was intended to keep the signal away from Greyhaven. This reframes the decision without proving that it was correct or successful.

Keep the captain unnamed until the project lead approves a name. Do not make him a hidden antagonist or confirm his body, survival or final location.

## 5. The Harbourmaster — keeper of the record

**External role:** harbour authority, channel watch and work assignment.  
**Private pressure:** maintain a safe harbour while the record of missing boats grows.  
**Defining habit:** returns to the watch log, checks the same markers and corrects older charts rather than trusting a remembered bearing.  
**Contradiction:** believes a good record matters, but knows it cannot explain every absence.

The Harbourmaster initially treats the Sounder as a new hand who must learn the channel. As the player returns reliably and respects warnings, the Harbourmaster allows more personal discussion about the old charts and the meaning of keeping a record. The character does not become a believer or an investigator; their final contribution is practical: make the warning legible to the next vessel.

**Visual / furniture language for later art:** a working desk with tide and watch books, sharpened pencils, repaired chart weights, a marked ruler, a mug repeatedly set in the same ring and a small stack of corrected charts. Keep it lived-in, not an office filled with mystical maps.

The Harbourmaster remains unnamed in the current runtime.

## 6. Mara Venn — the market that remembers

**External role:** fishmonger, buyer and keeper of market accounts.  
**Private pressure:** missing crews leave debts, unpaid ice and empty delivery spaces, while the market still has to operate.  
**Defining habit:** keeps a crate in its usual place for a crew that owes her for ice and refuses to turn their absence into gossip.  
**Contradiction:** speaks in practical terms but attaches emotional weight to apparently mundane records.

Mara first assesses the player through catch handling and honesty. Her observations about cold-centred fish are kept carefully apart from the ordinary work of checking storage. As trust grows, the player learns that she records the names behind unpaid accounts so the market does not forget who used to stand there.

**Proposed private hobby:** she sorts found sea glass and worn ceramic fragments by colour, thickness and the way the water has rounded their edges. She does not claim they predict anything; the habit gives her a quiet task after the market closes. This is a proposed character addition, not a claim that a dedicated collectible or hobby interaction exists in the prototype.

**Visual / furniture language for later art:** a clean weighing counter, chalk tally slate, covered ice box, one carefully kept empty crate, sorted jars of glass and ceramic, and a personal shelf that does not obstruct food handling.

## 7. Tomas Vale — the mechanic who listens twice

**External role:** boat mechanic and repair diagnostician.  
**Private pressure:** a repair signed off too early can send someone into danger.  
**Defining habit:** listens to the engine before opening the casing and repairs old radio receivers after the engine work is done.  
**Contradiction:** sceptical about supernatural explanations, yet fascinated by the difference between a genuine transmission and interference.

Tomas begins with ordinary mechanical causes. He asks the player to describe the engine honestly, preserves measurements and refuses to label an unexplained hull mark as proof of an animal. The more trust the player earns, the more willing he is to admit that an instrument can be in working order and still return something he cannot explain.

**Visual / furniture language for later art:** a scarred workbench, labelled bearing drawers, chalked fastener checks, dismantled receiver parts, hand tools arranged by task, a radio with its casing open and a notebook of repair attempts. His hobby is repair and fault tracing, not occult radio listening.

## 8. Inez Sarmiento — the line that holds

**External role:** net mender and keeper of gear condition.  
**Private pressure:** damaged evidence is often discarded before anyone understands what happened.  
**Defining habit:** checks the whole length of a net and the line beside a knot, not just the obvious break.  
**Contradiction:** patient and gentle in manner, but firm when someone rushes to a conclusion.

Inez teaches the player to preserve and examine the returned net. Her mother's lessons appear through work sayings and careful repetition. At higher trust, the player learns that Inez makes small woven birds from spare sound line. The craft is private, delicate and practical in its use of leftover material; it is not a ritual connected to the Choir.

**Visual / furniture language for later art:** a repaired net frame, hanging mesh samples, float-line spools sorted by wear, a small tray of knots, scissors and needles, and two or three line birds near a personal window or shelf.

## 9. The returned skipper

**External role:** sole survivor of one of the three boats that passed beyond the outer marker.  
**Private pressure:** fragmented memory of a voyage they cannot account for.  
**Defining trait:** repeatedly asks whether the lighthouse bell is still ringing.  
**Knowledge boundary:** what they saw and heard on their trip; not the objective meaning of those observations.

Keep the skipper's name, age, family and vessel identity open. Their testimony should change in detail only when a clear reason exists, such as rest, a prompt, a recovered object or a contradiction in the record. Do not use the character as a convenient lore terminal.

## 10. Future Lighthouse Keeper

The Lighthouse Keeper is a proposed supporting role, not part of the current four-resident runtime cast. If introduced, their function is maintenance history, light operation, records and the practical consequences of retiring the bell. They should be a careful witness to the building, not the person who explains the Choir.

The keeper's personal history, name, current employment and reason for remaining at the lighthouse all require separate design.

## 11. Missing crews

Each missing crew member should eventually be given at least two of the following: a work role, a routine, a relationship, a debt, a tool, a preferred route, a personal object or a remembered phrase. The player should understand that a missing person had a life without collecting a complete biography for everyone.

Do not introduce names casually across multiple docs. Once a missing crew identity is approved, record the canonical name, boat, role, relationships, stable content ID and evidence sources in one roster.

## 12. Relationship implementation boundaries

- Existing four NPC IDs, seven-tier trust values, Yarn node names and save contracts remain unchanged.
- Core clue availability remains neutral-or-better and must not depend on a random schedule coincidence.
- Personal habits, private keepsakes and additional dialogue can be trust-gated.
- The proposed hobbies above do not yet imply new UI interactions, inventory items, furniture builders or save state.
- A later furniture pass should express the habits through specific prop clusters, not a generic clutter kit.
