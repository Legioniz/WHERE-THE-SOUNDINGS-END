# Story Structure — Four Acts

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-12

## 1. Structure at a glance

The campaign moves from an ordinary working harbour to a deep-water mystery, then returns to the human consequences in Greyhaven. The escalation is not simply a sequence of larger monsters. It is a widening disagreement between what instruments report, what witnesses remember and what the player can physically verify.

| Act | Title | Approximate dramatic function | Regions |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Familiar Waters | Establish routine, stakes, first anomaly and personal connection | Greyhaven, The Shallows |
| II | Beyond the Coast | Expand the geography and prove the incidents have a history | Blackrock Reach, The Outer Shoals |
| III | The Deep | Reveal the Orison's intent and the limits of evidence | The Drowned Parish, The Hollow Trench approach |
| IV | The Last Sounding | Force a final choice and resolve the protagonist's emotional arc | The Hollow Trench, Greyhaven aftermath |

These are narrative regions and progression targets. Current Unity scene and travel coverage is narrower; do not infer that the entire campaign is playable.

## 2. Escalation model

Each act should progress through five modes:
1. **Routine:** normal work, navigation and resident life establish what is ordinary.
2. **Contradiction:** a single detail does not fit the expected physical explanation.
3. **Verification:** the player repeats, compares or physically investigates the clue.
4. **Reinterpretation:** a later clue changes what the earlier event could mean.
5. **Consequence:** the discovery alters a route, relationship, available evidence or final decision.

Avoid repeating the same scare with a louder sound. Each act must change the player's understanding.

## 3. Main story information gates

The campaign should use evidence families rather than one global clue counter:

- **Current incident evidence:** missing vessels, returned skipper, damaged hull/gear and sounding buoy.
- **Orison evidence:** survey record, route discrepancy, damaged instrument and crew procedure.
- **Historical evidence:** lighthouse bell maintenance, Drowned Parish record and an older sounding account.
- **Nell evidence:** the private call, radio fragments and final warning.
- **Geographical evidence:** repeated coordinates and the location where returns cease to behave reliably.

A critical revelation must have more than one route to understanding. If the player misses a single optional record, another witness or physical object should preserve the essential information.

## 4. Dramatic questions and act transitions

### Act I — Familiar Waters
Question: Can the player explain the missing boats without assuming a monster?
Transition: A verified repeated return and a private call-pattern connect the new incidents to the protagonist's old experience.

### Act II — Beyond the Coast
Question: Is the anomaly caused by local conditions, equipment, human interference or a wider phenomenon?
Transition: An older record proves related acoustic incidents were reported before the Orison disappeared.

### Act III — The Deep
Question: What did the Orison crew know, and why did they divert from their survey line?
Transition: The crew's records show they were attempting to keep the signal away from Greyhaven; Nell's final message was a warning to document rather than follow.

### Act IV — The Last Sounding
Question: What responsibility does the protagonist have when certainty is impossible?
Transition: The player chooses whether to follow the voice, cut the transmission chain or preserve and mark the evidence before leaving.

## 5. Pacing constraints

- Use calm intervals after major events. Fishing, repairs, selling and ordinary dialogue are part of the horror rhythm.
- Every major anomaly must be preceded by enough ordinary context for the player to notice the difference.
- Do not make the sea permanently hostile after the first act.
- Do not escalate every encounter into a chase or damage event.
- A safe return to Greyhaven should remain a meaningful option for most of the campaign.
- Keep the final expedition short enough that the player understands which resources and evidence matter.
- The narrative must remain comprehensible without reading every optional document.

## 6. Ending eligibility principles

Ending availability should be based on:
- a small set of essential evidence families;
- whether the player has understood the distinction between a voice and a living person;
- the final interaction with the sounding/radio system;
- the player's explicit final action.

There must be no hidden morality value, random ending roll, or surprise punishment for a compassionate choice. Optional evidence can deepen the aftermath and unlock the secret epilogue, but must not make a core ending impossible due to an obscure one-time event.

## 7. Vertical-slice boundary

The first vertical slice should not implement all four acts. It should demonstrate the Act I promise:
- a functioning departure and return loop;
- a Greyhaven job and a small investigation;
- one anomaly that can be checked rather than merely endured;
- one meaningful resident conversation;
- one record or object that connects routine maritime work to the wider mystery;
- persistence across save and reload.

The slice can foreshadow Nell and the Orison without revealing the captain's route decision or the final warning. No ending implementation is required for the first slice.
