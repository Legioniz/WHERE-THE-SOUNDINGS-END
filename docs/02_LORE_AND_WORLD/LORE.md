# Lore and Narrative Continuity Guide

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Working narrative design standard — proposals remain subject to review  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-14

## Purpose

The [World Bible](WORLD-BIBLE.md) owns setting facts. This guide owns the method: how a clue is classified, how a rumour differs from evidence, and how the player is allowed to reach an interpretation.

Lore should deepen the player's relationship with the sea and the people who work on it. It should not become a separate collectible checklist that interrupts the expedition loop.

## The five lore layers

| Layer | Main delivery | Typical material |
|---|---|---|
| Working life | Resident schedules, service spaces, shop records and boat routines | Catch ledgers, repair notes, borrowed tools, empty crates |
| Physical history | Architecture, wrecks, changing shorelines and reused materials | Old foundations, patched breakwaters, obsolete fittings |
| Oral memory | Stories repeated and altered by people | Bell sayings, route warnings, disputed accounts |
| Instrument evidence | Soundings, bearings, radio, weather and measurements | Logs, recorder fragments, chart corrections |
| Personal history | Objects, relationships and family memories | Letters, private calls, keepsakes, work habits |

A single object may support multiple layers, but it must not answer every layer at once.

## Evidence language

- **Rumour:** a statement repeated by someone who may not have witnessed the event.
- **Observation:** what a person directly saw, heard, measured or handled.
- **Record:** a dated or attributed artefact preserving an observation or decision.
- **Corroboration:** independent material that supports one specific claim.
- **Interpretation:** an explanation proposed by a witness or the player.
- **Inference:** a conclusion supported by several observations but not directly observed.
- **Unknown:** a question the available evidence cannot settle.

Do not label a claim "confirmed" merely because several people repeat the same original rumour.

## Current story vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Continuity rule |
|---|---|---|
| **The Sounder** | Production designation for the unnamed protagonist | Do not turn it into a spoken name or save identifier without approval |
| **Orison** | Survey vessel lost eight years before the present story | Preserve the captain / older sister Nell / apprentice Sounder relationship |
| **The Choir** | Human working label for recurring acoustic anomalies | Never write it as a confirmed species or omniscient mind |
| **Late return** | A sounding or acoustic response arriving outside the expected interval | A description, not a diagnosis |
| **Outer marker** | Local navigational boundary beyond which the three opening boats travelled | Do not invent exact coordinates before the world map is unified |
| **Drowned Parish** | Name used for submerged remains of an older settlement | Keep its physical history distinct from the lighthouse bell's service history |
| **Last Mark** | Recording a boundary and preserving evidence rather than following a signal | An ethical action, not a magic symbol |
| **Private call** | The sibling call-and-response pattern shared by Nell and the Sounder | Keep it specific; never use it as a generic scare sound |

## Clue authoring template

Every major clue should identify:
1. **Source:** who or what produced it.
2. **Acquisition:** where and how the player can obtain it.
3. **Observed detail:** the thing actually present.
4. **Reliability:** what can make the source incomplete or wrong.
5. **Alternative explanations:** at least one ordinary possibility where appropriate.
6. **Corroboration:** what independent clue can support or challenge it.
7. **Story use:** which question it changes.
8. **Access rule:** whether it is core, optional or relationship-dependent.
9. **Persistence:** stable evidence ID and save owner when implemented.
10. **Status:** proposed, authored, implemented, tested or verified.

## Character knowledge rules

- Harbourmaster: channel, watch, records, local incidents and bell maintenance history.
- Mara Venn: catch condition, storage, market debts, crew routines and the human cost of absence.
- Tomas Vale: machinery, hull damage, radio receivers and plausible equipment failure.
- Inez Sarmiento: line, knots, wear, mesh, float habits and gear handling.
- The Sounder: personal memories of the Orison, practical survey skills and emotional reactions, but no privileged explanation.
- Nell's records: only what Nell could know when she made them.
- The Orison Captain's records: route and command decisions, not the complete meaning of the phenomenon.
- Returned skipper: a limited account shaped by conditions and memory of one trip.

No single witness possesses the whole story.

## Presentation rules

- Prefer evidence that changes how an earlier scene is understood.
- Avoid long documents that merely repeat facts already stated in dialogue.
- Keep document voices specific: a mechanic writes measurements; a market ledger records dates and weights; a captain records decisions; a frightened letter may be incomplete.
- Distinguish original text from later annotations.
- An environmental prop may suggest a story without writing a quest flag or journal entry.
- Do not hide essential information only in an audio cue; provide captions or a readable equivalent where the cue is required for progression.
- A clue should not provide a free compass, GPS fix or hidden map mark in unvisited water.

## Continuity checks

Before approving new lore, ask:
- Does it contradict the eleven-year bell gap or eight-year Orison gap?
- Does it collapse the Choir into a confirmed creature or a single convenient explanation?
- Does it give a character knowledge outside their job, memory or source?
- Does it imply that all unusual wildlife is hostile or anomalous?
- Does it turn the Drowned Parish's bell into the lighthouse bell without evidence?
- Does it make the protagonist's guilt an objective cause of the Orison loss?
- Does it remove a player's ability to test, doubt, leave or return?
- Does it introduce a religion, language, culture or real-world location that has not been approved?
- Does the new clue have an independent route if it is needed for progression?
