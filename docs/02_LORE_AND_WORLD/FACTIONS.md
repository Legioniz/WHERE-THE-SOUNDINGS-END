# Factions, Institutions and Working Networks

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed social-world framework — no antagonist faction established  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-14

## 1. Design direction

Greyhaven is held together by work, obligation and local trust, not by a collection of rival secret societies. The term **faction** is used here for institutions and informal working networks. These groups are not combat teams, universal reputation tracks or automatic quest gates.

A resident may belong to more than one network. Disagreement should come from different responsibilities, risks and information—not from a simplistic good-versus-evil split.

## 2. The Harbour Office and Watch

**Function:** channel records, moorings, local warnings, weather observations, harbour safety and routine work assignment.  
**Current face:** the unnamed Harbourmaster.  
**Resources:** watch logs, local charts, channel markers, incident reports and access to harbour working spaces.  
**Values:** safe return, reliable records, visible responsibility and not sending another vessel into a route that cannot be described.  
**Internal tension:** the Harbourmaster must keep the harbour functioning while residents want answers about missing boats. A cautious record can look like indifference to someone waiting for a relative.  
**Knowledge boundary:** the office can establish dates, watch observations and local navigation facts. It cannot explain the Orison or identify the source of the Choir.

## 3. The Market Circle

**Function:** fish grading, ice orders, storage, pricing, informal credit and exchange between crews and buyers.  
**Current face:** Mara Venn.  
**Resources:** market ledgers, cold storage, scales, supplier relationships and knowledge of which crews normally land where.  
**Values:** honest handling, fair weights, reliable delivery and preserving the dignity of people who work the water.  
**Internal tension:** the market needs stock and cash flow, but sensational rumours can damage sales and turn missing crews into gossip. Mara's practice of keeping a place for an unpaid crew is both an account and a refusal to erase them.  
**Knowledge boundary:** the Market Circle can identify patterns in catch condition, freshness, supply and crew routine. It cannot diagnose an acoustic signal.

## 4. The Repair and Gear Network

**Function:** engine work, hull inspection, fittings, line repair, net maintenance, salvaged material and the informal exchange of practical knowledge.  
**Current faces:** Tomas Vale and Inez Sarmiento.  
**Resources:** repair benches, spare parts, worn components, gear samples, maintenance notes and craft knowledge.  
**Values:** evidence over guesswork, repairability, safe return and not sending damaged equipment back to sea.  
**Internal tension:** Tomas prefers a measurable mechanical explanation; Inez insists the entire length of a damaged net be preserved before anyone declares a cause. They can disagree about what evidence is sufficient without either being foolish.  
**Knowledge boundary:** they can establish physical condition and plausible failure modes. Neither should interpret the Choir as an expert.

## 5. The Survey Service — provisional institution

**Function:** historical charting, bathymetric work and operation of survey vessels such as the Orison.  
**Status:** the Orison's sponsor and formal institutional name are undecided.

The survey service may have maintained technical standards, chart archives, instrument logs and a professional chain of command. It should not become a secret state project, corporation conspiracy or villain organisation without explicit approval.

**Current narrative use:** its records explain why the Orison existed and why technical documents survive beyond the local harbour. The captain's decisions are personal command decisions under uncertain conditions, not proof of an institutional plot.

## 6. Parish memory network — informal

People connected to the Drowned Parish preserve names, reused objects, copied records and conflicting accounts of the lower settlement. This network is not yet a formal archive or a hereditary priesthood.

Its members may disagree about:
- whether the old district was abandoned gradually or after one memorable storm;
- which building was the bell house;
- whether a bell-like sound is a real memory or a story repeated by later generations;
- which salvaged objects truly came from the Parish.

Do not introduce a single keeper who possesses the whole history. Records should be distributed and sometimes incomplete.

## 7. Missing crews and mutual obligations

Boat crews form a practical network through shared launches, equipment loans, ice accounts, repairs, family connections and weather decisions. They do not need an official guild name.

The opening incident involves three boats beyond the outer marker, one returned and two missing. The missing people should be individuated through ordinary responsibilities and relationships. Their families and workmates should not exist only to deliver exposition.

## 8. Relationship and quest rules

- Individual resident trust remains separate; there is no global faction reputation.
- A player can earn trust through actions relevant to a group's responsibilities.
- Critical story evidence must remain obtainable even if the player has low trust.
- Services, prices and quest access may use existing per-resident relationships where the owning system supports them.
- Do not add a new faction manager solely to represent these groups.
- New named organisations, leaders, symbols, uniforms and political conflicts require project-lead review.
