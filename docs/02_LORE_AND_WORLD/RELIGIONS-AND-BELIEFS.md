# Religions, Faith and Everyday Belief

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed cultural framework — no formal religion or pantheon locked  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-14

## 1. Scope and restraint

The setting does not yet have an approved real-world location, language, population history or formal religious tradition. This document therefore defines how faith and belief can appear without inventing a direct stand-in for a living religion.

Greyhaven is not a monolith. Residents may be religious, privately spiritual, sceptical, indifferent or unwilling to discuss belief. They share a working coastline and obligations; they do not need to share one doctrine.

No named god, sacred being, church hierarchy, prayer text, burial rite or religious calendar is currently established as canon. Those choices require a deliberate cultural and narrative review.

## 2. Everyday belief is not the same as religion

- **Faith:** a person's religious or spiritual commitments.
- **Folk belief:** an informal explanation or inherited saying, often different between families.
- **Work custom:** a repeated practice with practical value, such as checking a knot, counting moorings or leaving a chart margin.
- **Superstition:** a belief that a particular action or sign changes the risk of a voyage.
- **Public record:** what the harbour or market can establish about an event.
- **Personal grief:** a private way of remembering someone who has not returned.

Do not convert every work custom into a ritual or every rumour into an organised belief system.

## 3. A plural harbour

The harbour contains people with different ways of interpreting risk:
- the Harbourmaster trusts watch logs, repaired markers and repeatable observations;
- Mara values the names and obligations behind each delivery;
- Tomas begins with equipment and mechanical explanations;
- Inez trusts the physical condition of line and the sequence of work;
- other residents may rely on family stories, personal faith, local sayings or no explanation at all.

These are character directions, not a complete demographic statement about the island.

## 4. Remembering the missing

The current Greyhaven story already contains a small human practice: Mara keeps an unpaid crate associated with a missing crew. It is not a shrine or magical container. It is a daily market choice that keeps the crew's names in a practical record.

Other future memorial practices may include a name added to a harbour ledger, a repaired object kept by a family, or a small marker placed at a safe public location. These are proposals and should be authored through individual characters rather than declared a universal island ritual.

Do not turn an absence into a supernatural collectible. A memorial should first honour the person being remembered.

## 5. Beliefs around the sea

The local stories in [Ocean Mythology](OCEAN-MYTHOLOGY.md) are deliberately plural:
- some crews avoid steering toward an isolated light without a second reference;
- some people repeat the story of a bell beneath the Drowned Parish;
- some tell the Empty Net story as a warning against overconfidence;
- some treat the Choir as a useful label for confusing reports; others reject the name.

No belief should be presented as objectively correct merely because it appears in several conversations. Corroborated evidence should remain separate from faith and superstition.

## 6. Writing and representation rules

1. Do not use a real-world sacred figure as an enemy, loot source, collectible or boss without appropriate research, consultation and permission.
2. Do not invent a single "island religion" that everyone follows.
3. Do not use prayer, burial, shrine or sacred-space imagery as generic spooky decoration.
4. Attribute any real-world inspiration to a specific community and source in the research documents.
5. Keep character beliefs personal and consistent; do not change them just to deliver a clue.
6. Allow sincere faith and practical scepticism to coexist without mocking either.
7. Distinguish supernatural interpretation from a person's actual religious identity.
8. Do not add a faith mechanic or belief-based stat without a separate gameplay design.

## Open decisions

The project lead may later approve a particular historical or contemporary religious presence, a fictional local practice, a memorial tradition or a formal belief institution. Until then, use individual voices and ordinary customs rather than a universal doctrine.
