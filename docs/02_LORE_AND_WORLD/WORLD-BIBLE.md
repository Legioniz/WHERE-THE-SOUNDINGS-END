# World Bible

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed narrative foundation — core mystery intentionally unresolved  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.5  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-14

## 1. The promise of the setting

An isolated island community lives by the water it can read. Boats leave before dawn, fish are weighed, engines are opened, nets are repaired, charts are corrected, and people wait for vessels that may be late. Beyond the protected harbour, the same sea becomes harder to interpret. A depth return arrives twice. A familiar voice appears on an empty frequency. A bell is heard where no bell is ringing.

**WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END** is about the human cost of trying to turn uncertainty into certainty. The player is not chosen to defeat an ancient evil. They are a working mariner who must decide what can be known, what must be recorded, and when continuing to listen becomes irresponsible.

The world should remain recognisably physical. Weather, geology, equipment, ecology, fatigue, memory, economics and human error all matter. The unexplained is unsettling because the ordinary world has first been made legible.

## 2. Canon and evidence policy

This document is the setting's central continuity reference. It does not automatically make every proposal final canon.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Prototype-established** | Present in current source or dialogue; still subject to in-game verification |
| **Working baseline** | Repeated consistently across current design documents; use unless deliberately revised |
| **Proposed** | Authored direction awaiting project-lead review |
| **Rumour / belief** | Something a person or community says; not necessarily true |
| **Observed** | Something directly seen, heard, measured or handled |
| **Corroborated** | Independent evidence supports a limited part of an observation |
| **Unknown** | Available evidence does not settle the question |

The central events involving the Orison and the Choir remain **Proposed**. Their emotional direction is coherent, but their exact cause, ontology, final chronology and endings are not locked. Real-world folklore research is inspiration only. It does not establish fictional history or supernatural truth.

### Prototype-established foundations

- Greyhaven is the opening harbour and dependable return point.
- The active resident cast consists of the unnamed Harbourmaster, Mara Venn, Tomas Vale and Inez Sarmiento. Preserve their stable runtime identities, work roles, schedules and relationship-reactive dialogue.
- Greyhaven's current evidence dialogue records four separate observations: the silent lighthouse bell's log history, cold-centred catches, unexplained hull scoring, and an empty but untorn net.
- The protagonist is a working designation, **the Sounder**. Name, gender, face and exact age remain undecided.
- The six-region scaffold is Greyhaven, The Shallows, Blackrock Reach, The Drowned Parish, The Outer Shoals and The Hollow Trench.
- The current Unity project is a connected prototype. The six-region travel network, complete campaign, walkable on-foot interiors and story quest state are not yet implemented.

## 3. The sea and the island chain

The setting occupies an unnamed island chain. The world is not assigned a real-world country, exact latitude, language group or single cultural analogue. Its working physical model is a volcanic island arc with a sheltered bay, exposed basalt coast, shallow shelf, drowned lowland, offshore ridges and a deep tectonic margin.

The warm-water / tropical-to-subtropical climate in the ecology documents is provisional. Final climate, island dimensions, currents, species ranges and travel distances require a coherent world-map pass. Do not add exact bearings or nautical miles until the regions share an authored coordinate model.

The sea contains several interacting layers:

1. **Physical water:** tide, swell, wind, current, visibility, depth, temperature, salinity and bottom type.
2. **Living water:** feeding, migration, shelter, breeding, predation, scavenging and recovery.
3. **Working water:** channels, moorings, charts, trade routes, fishing grounds, salvage and local knowledge.
4. **Remembered water:** ship losses, old routes, records, family histories and stories repeated by people who were not present.
5. **Unexplained water:** acoustic contradictions and rare events that remain distinct from normal ecology.

The layers can overlap, but one must not automatically explain another. A whale call is not a Choir event because it sounds strange. A damaged net is not proof of a monster. A legendary animal does not become supernatural merely because it is large.

## 4. The six-region identity

| Region | Identity | Narrative purpose | Player lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Greyhaven** | Sheltered volcanic bay, estuary, village, lighthouse and working harbour | Establish the human community, missing boats and first contradictory records | Routine is valuable evidence; a safe return matters |
| **The Shallows** | Seagrass, reef flats, sand channels and clear shelf water | Establish ordinary soundings and healthy marine life | Learn what normal water looks and sounds like |
| **Blackrock Reach** | Exposed basalt headlands, surge channels, caves and difficult coast | Introduce natural acoustic reflections and exposed navigation | A strange return may have a physical cause |
| **The Outer Shoals** | Migratory grounds, offshore ridges, open horizons and long crossings | Connect distant incidents and test route discipline | Similar evidence in different places is not yet proof of one cause |
| **The Drowned Parish** | Remains of an older lowland settlement beneath changing water | Preserve human history older than the protagonist's loss | Records outlive their makers, but records are incomplete |
| **The Hollow Trench** | Deep slope, canyon margin, seep and brine features, then the last reliable sounding boundary | Bring the Orison evidence and final choice together | There are limits to what a measurement or voice can promise |

These are narrative and ecological identities, not proof that all six regions currently exist as connected playable scenes. The Outer Shoals and Hollow Trench survey sandboxes are separate prototypes, not an inter-region sailing network.

## 5. Greyhaven and its people

Greyhaven is a working settlement, not a village built around a secret cult. Its people have ordinary reasons to disagree: money, safety, maintenance, family obligation, pride, old disputes and differing standards of evidence.

The Harbour Office watches the channel. The market depends on fair handling and reliable deliveries. The repair and net yards keep vessels and gear in service. The inn provides a place for crews to wait, argue and hear rumours. Houses show that residents have lives outside their service roles.

The four current residents each notice a different part of the world:

- **The Harbourmaster** trusts records, watch routines, tide notes and corrected charts. A chart is a record of what was observed, not a promise about what the water will do.
- **Mara Venn** judges catch by condition and handling. She still sets aside a crate for a crew that owes her for ice; the ledger keeps their names present without pretending they will return.
- **Tomas Vale** listens to engines and repairs radio receivers when the machinery is quiet. He wants to distinguish a real transmission from interference before he gives either one a name.
- **Inez Sarmiento** preserves damaged line long enough to inspect it. Her mother taught her to check a knot and the rope beside it; her small woven birds made from spare line are a private craft, not a ritual.

Their personal habits are not supernatural powers. Their occupations determine what they can credibly observe. Core evidence must not be hidden behind high relationship scores; trust opens personal context, not exclusive access to the main plot.

## 6. Central mystery — the Choir

**The Choir** is the working name used by people comparing a family of recurring acoustic anomalies. It is a label for a pattern, not the confirmed name of an animal, god, species, intelligence or organisation.

Its reported signs include:
- a second sounding return arriving after the expected return;
- repeated measurements that disagree despite an apparently stable vessel and instrument;
- an altered bearing or depth that cannot be reconciled with the available chart;
- radio phrases or voice contours resembling a previously heard speaker;
- bell-like tones where no operating bell is present;
- a brief reduction or absence of ordinary ambient sound.

The Orison record describes a second return arriving eleven seconds after the first. That detail is a specific incident, not a universal timer for every Choir event.

The four Greyhaven observations are separate witness records. The cold-centred catch, hull scoring and untorn net may be related, unrelated or only partly related to the current missing-boat incident. Their value comes from independent observation, not from an automatic shared explanation.

### The central narrative proposal

Eight years before the opening, the survey vessel **Orison** was lost near waters later associated with the Hollow Trench. The protagonist was an apprentice sounder aboard it. Their father was the captain; their older sister **Nell** was the radio operator. The protagonist was the only person recovered.

The lighthouse bell had already been silent for eleven years when the present story begins. Three years after the last confirmed bell sounding, the Orison was lost. In the present, three boats pass beyond the outer marker; one returns. The returned skipper repeatedly asks whether the lighthouse bell is still ringing.

The proposed story suggests that the Orison crew began limiting transmissions and changed course to reduce the chance of drawing the recurring signal toward Greyhaven. Nell's final message is reconstructed from separate records as:

**"Don't follow the sound. Mark the water."**

The line means: preserve the position and evidence; do not mistake a familiar signal for a living person who can be reached by following it.

## 7. The Orison family and the emotional spine

The Sounder feels responsible because they answered a call that resembled Nell's private sibling signal. That guilt is emotionally real but is not proof that an apprentice caused the vessel's loss. Their father made command decisions under uncertainty. Nell acted as a competent radio operator with limited time and information. The crew's exact final sequence and individual fates remain unresolved.

The story must give each family member an ordinary life beyond the incident:
- Nell should be remembered through humour, impatience, technical competence and familiar habits as well as the warning.
- The captain should be protective and imperfect, not a secret villain whose every decision was correct.
- The Sounder should remain a practical mariner, not become a combat hero or a supernatural interpreter.

A voice alone must never confirm Nell's survival. The game may establish what the crew intended without claiming certainty about what happened to every person.

## 8. Themes and recurring motifs

| Motif | Literal function | Emotional meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Sounding pulse | Measures depth and can reveal a site | The desire to know what is below |
| Delayed return | A measurable contradiction | Memory arriving changed |
| Lighthouse bell | A navigational warning now absent from service | A community's missing reassurance |
| Parish bell stories | A separate layer of local memory | The past persisting beneath ordinary routes |
| Chart margin | Space for an uncertain observation | Honesty about what is not known |
| Empty crate | A market place kept for a missing crew | Grief expressed through routine |
| Repaired radio | A practical machine and a possible source of interference | The wish to distinguish signal from noise |
| Woven line bird | A small craft made from spare working material | Care and continuity without explanation |
| Quiet water | A rare change in the soundscape | The fear of losing the ordinary world |
| The old boat | The protagonist's livelihood | Returning to work without returning to the past |

## 9. Lore delivery principles

1. Let routine, work and ecology establish a baseline before an anomaly appears.
2. Separate what was observed from what a witness believes it means.
3. Give important revelations more than one evidence route.
4. Let records disagree for identifiable reasons: date, instrument condition, weather, memory, incentives or incomplete observation.
5. Make local myths human-scale and varied; do not give every resident the same superstition.
6. Do not make every legendary encounter part of the Choir.
7. Keep silence, distance, omission and absence as expressive tools.
8. Never use a required lore clue as the sole delivery of an essential audio cue.
9. Do not make an obscure collectible or random NPC schedule a progression lock.
10. Allow the player to leave a question unresolved without being treated as foolish.

## 10. Open decisions requiring project-lead review

- The island chain's real-world inspiration, climate, language and exact geographical placement.
- The exact date, circumstances and human decision that ended lighthouse bell service.
- The Drowned Parish's settlement chronology, former population and physical reasons for its submergence.
- Whether the Orison was privately funded, publicly commissioned or part of a wider survey service.
- The names and individual stories of the missing crews and returned skipper.
- The final sequence and fate of Nell, the captain and the Orison crew.
- The physical, acoustic or supernatural explanation—if any—behind the Choir.
- Whether any ending receives an explicit epilogue beyond the three proposed core endings.

Until these decisions are approved, downstream content must preserve the uncertainty described here.
