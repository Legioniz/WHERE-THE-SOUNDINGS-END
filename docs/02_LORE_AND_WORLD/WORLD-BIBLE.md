# World Bible

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Working baseline with proposed narrative additions  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.3  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-12

An isolated island community depends on surrounding fishing waters. A number of fishing boats have disappeared or returned under unexplained circumstances. The protagonist takes work connected to these incidents and discovers signs of an older, poorly understood relationship between village and sea. The ocean is a connected system of ecology, physical spaces, memory, acoustic signals and entities; extraction and disturbance can change what is encountered.

## Regions

- Greyhaven: home village, harbour, service hub and opening routine.
- The Shallows: accessible fishing grounds and learning space.
- Blackrock Reach: exposed waters and geological hazards.
- The Drowned Parish: submerged settlement remains and historical evidence.
- The Outer Shoals: remote migratory grounds and difficult navigation.
- The Hollow Trench: deep-water endgame region.

## Geography and ecological baseline

The working geological model is a volcanic island arc with a sheltered bay, shallow island shelf, exposed volcanic coastline, drowned river valley, offshore ridges, submarine canyons and a deep tectonic margin. This is a design foundation, not a locked real-world coordinate or final tectonic reconstruction.

The provisional ecological model includes estuary and mangrove-edge habitats, seagrass meadows, coral and rocky reefs, intertidal shelves, pelagic migration corridors, seamount-like hardgrounds, deep-sea canyon communities, cold seeps, hydrothermal vents and occasional large food falls. Climate and exact regional species composition remain unapproved.

Wildlife is not synonymous with hostility. Most animals feed, migrate, rest, shelter, reproduce, scavenge or avoid the boat. Player-directed attacks require an authored ecological or anomalous reason. Regional distribution and local fauna are documented separately.

## Provisional setting facts

- Fishermen and boats have disappeared or returned under unexplained circumstances.
- The lighthouse bell has been silent for eleven years at the start of the proposed main story.
- The returned skipper from the opening incident asks whether the bell is still ringing.
- The Sounding Rig supplies evidence, never omniscient answers.
- Village beliefs conflict; practical observations should be separated from interpretation.
- The Drowned Parish preserves historical evidence older than the protagonist's personal loss.

## Proposed central narrative mystery — the Choir

> **Status: Proposed; not locked canon.**

The Choir is the working name for a recurring family of acoustic anomalies associated with particular waters, soundings and historical incidents. The name is a human label for a pattern, not proof of a creature, intelligence or supernatural species.

Observed forms may include delayed sounding returns, unstable depth/bearing readings, repeated radio calls, familiar voice contours, bell-like tones where no bell is operating, and abrupt changes in the ambient soundscape. A voice must never be treated as proof that its speaker is alive.

The proposed main story links the present missing boats to the survey vessel Orison, lost eight years before the opening. The protagonist was an apprentice sounder aboard the vessel; their older sister Nell worked the radio and their father was captain. The protagonist was the only person recovered. Evidence later suggests the crew diverted away from Greyhaven to avoid drawing the signal toward the harbour. Nell's final message was a warning to mark the location rather than follow the voice.

The exact cause, awareness, physical mechanism and origin of the Choir remain intentionally unresolved. Legendary creatures and ordinary marine ecology are separate systems and must not automatically be explained as manifestations of the Choir.

## Canon not yet locked

Exact geography, chronology, climate, species composition, supernatural ontology, the cause of disappearances, protagonist identity, Nell's fate, the Orison crew's final moments and the relationship between the bell and the Choir remain subject to owner approval. Treat the Choir, Orison history and proposed ending truth as narrative proposals until approved.
