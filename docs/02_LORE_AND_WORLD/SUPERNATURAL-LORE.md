# Supernatural Lore — The Choir and the Limits of Evidence

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed anomaly bible — cause and ontology unresolved  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-14

## 1. Definition

**The Choir** is a human working label for recurring acoustic anomalies reported in particular waters and historical incidents. It is not a confirmed creature, collective mind, deity, curse, intelligent ocean or species.

The word exists because different witnesses have compared sounds and records. Some people use it seriously; others dislike the name because it makes separate events sound like one thing.

The game may establish recurring patterns without explaining their ultimate source. Consistency comes from observable limits and evidence rules, not from a definitive monster biography.

## 2. Observable forms

| Form | What a character can observe | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Delayed sounding | A second return follows the expected seabed return; the Orison record describes an eleven-second delay | That every event uses the same interval or that a living thing produced it |
| Unstable return | Repeated pings give a different depth, bearing or confidence under apparently stable conditions | That the instrument is infallible or the sea is consciously changing |
| Repeated radio material | A phrase, voice contour or call pattern appears on a receiver | That the original speaker is alive or transmitting now |
| False bell tone | A bell-like sound is heard where no bell is in service | That the lighthouse bell or Parish bell physically rang |
| Ambient subtraction | Several ordinary sound layers briefly fall away | That time has stopped, the player is unconscious or a creature is nearby |
| Physical discrepancy | Separate records describe unusual catch condition, hull marks or gear returns | That all such observations share a single cause |

## 3. Competing explanations

The player should be able to consider several possibilities:
- **Ordinary propagation:** reflections, refraction, absorption, multipath effects, changing temperature/salinity layers, bottom geometry and biological sound.
- **Equipment failure:** transducer damage, loose connections, poor calibration, power instability, radio interference or recording artefacts.
- **Human activity:** another vessel, an old transmitter, a buoy, a hidden recording, deliberate interference or a mistaken report.
- **Memory and expectation:** fatigue, stress, grief, misheard phrases and the human tendency to identify familiar patterns.
- **Unresolved anomaly:** the surviving evidence may not fit any one ordinary explanation.

These explanations are not all true in the same incident. New evidence may rule out one explanation for a specific observation without proving another.

## 4. Consistency rules

1. **Bound the geography.** Important events occur in authored waters, locations, conditions or investigation contexts. The Choir does not occur as a random explanation for every strange event.
2. **Respect source material.** A repeated phrase or voice should have a plausible source in a prior recording, transmission, overheard call or established memory. Do not use the anomaly to reveal arbitrary private facts.
3. **No mind reading.** The Choir is not confirmed to read thoughts, predict decisions or know what the player has not encountered.
4. **No universal imitation.** It does not automatically reproduce any voice at any time. The story must preserve uncertainty about how familiar material appears.
5. **No omniscience.** The signal does not explain the past, identify every missing person, name hidden locations or deliver objective quest instructions.
6. **No automatic hostility.** Hearing an anomaly does not itself trigger a chase, damage or attack. Threats require their own eligibility, warning and counterplay.
7. **No ecological shortcut.** Ordinary fish, whales, rays, sharks and other wildlife retain biological behaviour unless an individual encounter has a separately authored reason to be anomalous.
8. **Silence is limited.** Ambient subtraction is brief, authored and readable. Critical controls and hazard feedback remain available.
9. **Measurements stay fallible.** The Sounding Rig supplies evidence and confidence, not omniscient truth. A repeated reading can improve confidence without resolving the anomaly.
10. **A voice is not a person.** No voice-only event confirms a speaker's survival, death, location or present intention.

## 5. The Orison signature

The Orison's best-known technical detail is a second sounding return eleven seconds after the first. The crew reported inconsistent depth and bearing readings, then radio material resembling a private call shared by the Sounder and Nell.

The exact equipment, frequency, pulse shape, transducer arrangement and original recording are not yet specified. Do not invent precise technical specifications in dialogue until the Sounding Rig technical design has been reviewed.

The protagonist remembers the private call as personal. The player should hear enough to recognise its emotional weight, but the story should not repeat it as a generic sound effect.

## 6. Relationship to other legendary events

The legendary-encounter catalogue contains animal sightings, phantom vessels, currents, old stories and uncertain reports. They are separate content families.

- A large animal is not the Choir.
- A phantom ship is not automatically a signal projection.
- A natural sound is not a paranormal event because a frightened witness misidentified it.
- A real deep-sea phenomenon can inspire a fictional encounter without becoming supernatural.
- A local story may remain a story even after the player visits the place it describes.

Connect one of these systems to the Choir only when a specific authored clue supports that connection.

## 7. Player experience and pacing

- Ordinary sound should be present and recognisable for long stretches.
- The first contradiction should be subtle enough that a player can test it.
- Repetition should be meaningful, not simply louder.
- Offer practical counterchecks: change heading, stop the engine, compare a landmark, repeat a pulse, inspect equipment, ask a different witness or return to harbour.
- Do not punish a player for retreating from an uncertain event.
- Do not use unexplained warning flashes as a hidden lore delivery method. The existing non-directional warning flash remains intentionally unlabelled and without a note, map mark or gameplay effect.
- Keep the protected harbour a reliable refuge.

## 8. Journal and evidence wording

Preferred:
- "The return arrived eleven seconds after the first."
- "The bearing changed between two stationary checks."
- "The recording contains a phrase resembling Nell's private call."
- "The source has not been identified."

Avoid:
- "The Choir called the player."
- "Nell is alive inside the signal."
- "The sea wants the boat."
- "The creature copied the captain's soul."

The latter statements may appear as a character's rumour or emotional interpretation only when clearly attributed to that character.

## 9. Open questions

The game has not decided whether the recurring events share a physical mechanism, whether an entity is involved, whether the radio material is recorded or reconstructed, whether the Parish bell has ever been heard underwater, or what happened to the Orison crew. The most important design requirement is that each new clue be consistent with what the player has already observed while leaving more than one interpretation possible.
