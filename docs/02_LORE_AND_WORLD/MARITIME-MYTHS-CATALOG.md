|---|---|---|---|---|

## Natural phenomena suitable for grounded horror

These are real categories of ocean or atmospheric phenomena. Their presence in the game should be based on appropriate scientific references and should not imply that natural processes are supernatural.

- **Bioluminescence:** living organisms produce light; useful for brief, localized signals and misleading visual orientation.
- **Internal waves:** waves that travel within the ocean along density boundaries; a basis for strange underwater movement or shifting acoustic conditions.
- **Rogue waves:** unusually large waves can occur; use sparingly as a rare, telegraphed hazard rather than a random punishment.
- **Whale vocalizations:** complex sounds can travel across water; useful for acoustic navigation and ambiguity.
- **Hydrothermal vents:** seafloor systems that release heated, chemically altered water; suitable for a remote region with distinct ecology.
- **Brine pools:** dense, very salty water can collect in depressions and form striking underwater boundaries; useful as a hazardous deep-water landmark.
- **Harmful algal blooms / red tides:** some blooms discolor water and may produce toxins; not all red-colored water is a harmful bloom.
- **Fog and mirages:** atmospheric conditions can alter visibility and apparent distance; use as navigational uncertainty, not unexplained magic by default.

## Implementation principles

1. Let the player learn a rule before the rule becomes dangerous.
2. Use sound, current, visibility, and environmental evidence as clues, not just decoration.
3. Give rare events a readable eligibility condition and a fair warning.
4. Avoid turning every tradition into a creature encounter. Some should remain stories, architecture, taboos, maps, or human explanations.
5. Every quest proposal must declare its source inspiration, fictional transformation, cultural review needs, system dependencies, and canon status.

## Expanded research dossier

The detailed, source-traceable expansion is now maintained in [Ocean Legends and Sea Monsters](OCEAN-LEGENDS-AND-SEA-MONSTERS.md). It covers the *Essex* / Mocha Dick history behind *Moby-Dick*, medieval Norse hafgufa and lyngbakr, the Kraken, Leviathan, Jörmungandr, Scylla and Charybdis, the Flying Dutchman, Davy Jones, Japanese sea apparitions and deities, Māori and Inuit traditions, Philippine Bakunawa and kataw accounts, Caribbean Lusca, selkies, and sea-serpent reports.

The expansion separates historical events, historical testimony, literary fiction, mythic / religious texts, living oral traditions, and scientific comparisons. It includes original links and cultural-use cautions. It remains non-canon.
