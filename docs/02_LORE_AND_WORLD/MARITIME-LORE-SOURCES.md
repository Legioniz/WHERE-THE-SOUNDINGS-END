## Primary-source directions for the next research pass

- **Greek literary traditions:** translations and scholarly commentaries on Homer's *Odyssey*, Apollodorus, and later classical sources; distinguish the older bird-like siren tradition from later mermaid depictions.
- **Welsh submerged-land traditions:** Welsh-language folklore collections, national library / archive materials, and scholarship on Cantre'r Gwaelod and Cardigan Bay.
- **Japanese folklore:** academic folklore collections and Japanese museum or cultural-institution resources for Umibōzu and maritime yōkai.
- **Scottish and Irish selkie stories:** regional folklore archives and critical scholarship on local variants.
- **Māori, Hawaiian, Inuit, African, and Aboriginal Australian traditions:** prioritize community-authored or community-approved material and consultation. Do not infer universal practices from a single summary.
- **Marine science:** NOAA, national oceanographic institutions, peer-reviewed oceanography, and university research for bioluminescence, internal waves, whale acoustics, harmful algal blooms, hydrothermal vents, brine pools, fog, and rogue waves.

## Bibliography maintenance

For each source added later, record author / institution, title, publication date, source type, URL or stable identifier, access date, specific claims supported, and any limitations. Do not cite a source merely because it mentions the same mythic name.
