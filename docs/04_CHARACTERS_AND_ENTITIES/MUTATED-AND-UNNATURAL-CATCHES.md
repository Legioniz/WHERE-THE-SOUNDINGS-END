# Mutated and Unnatural Catchable Specimens

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed creature and fishing content — not final canon  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.1  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-10  
> Scope: Rare abnormal wildlife, mutated specimen variants, anomalous catchables, capture conditions, ecology, risk, and research-market rules

## 1. Design intent

The archipelago should contain more than ordinary fish and obvious monsters. Some catches should look wrong but remain biologically understandable. Others should be impossible in ways that the player can learn through repeated observation.

This roster uses three separate classifications:

1. **Aberrant specimens (ABR)** — individual animals with unusual pigmentation, injury, deformity, parasite burden, or other abnormal traits. They are not automatically a new species, a supernatural creature, or proof of pollution.
2. **Unnatural specimens (UNC)** — fictional entities whose behavior or anatomy violates an established rule of the world. They may be catchable, but are not ordinary food.
3. **Major anomalous threats** — entities such as the Sleeper, Glassback, Cliffblind, and Countercurrent. These remain governed by Anomalous Species and Site-Specific Entities; do not turn every catchable specimen into a boss.

The normal catalog remains 100 fish and 47 invertebrate entries. The entries below are separate variants and discoveries; they do not inflate the ordinary species count.

## 2. Real-world biological foundation and limits

Wild fish can show unusual pigmentation, including partial or complete albinism and other pigment aberrations. These may have genetic, congenital, dietary, or environmental explanations; they are not all disease and are not automatically unsafe to eat. Fish can also survive spinal deformities such as scoliosis and kyphosis. A study of flathead species associated marked spinal curvature with a Myxobolus-like parasite, while other reports discuss injury, nutrition, and genetic factors as possible causes. These examples are inspiration, not proof that the fictional archipelago's specimens share one cause.

Deep-sea animals also provide extraordinary but real reference points: anglerfish use luminous lures associated with symbiotic bacteria, parasitic males occur in some anglerfish lineages, and biofluorescence has been documented in a Pacific footballfish. Real biology should remain stranger than generic fantasy, but we must not call these known adaptations mutations.

**Research references**
- Alaska Department of Fish and Game, [Diseases of Wild and Cultured Fishes in Alaska — Pigment Aberrations](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/static/species/disease/pdfs/fish_disease_book.pdf).
- Ngo et al. (2024), [Scoliosis and kyphosis in flathead fish associated with a Myxobolus acanthogobii-like parasite](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38327021/).
- Jawad et al. (2018), [Vertebral column deformity in adult wild haddock](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30294909/).
- Ludt & Clardy (2022), [First detection of biofluorescence in a deep-sea anglerfish](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34982469/).
- Baker et al. (2019), [Luminous bacterial symbionts in deep-sea anglerfishes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6773444/).
- Brownstein et al. (2024), [Synergistic innovations enabled the radiation of anglerfishes in the deep open ocean](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.04.066).

Do not infer a single cause from appearance. In-game researchers should use cautious language such as "consistent with," "suspected," and "cause unknown."

## 3. Aberrant specimens — unusual but biologically interpretable

These are rare individuals or local morphs of existing species. They do not receive new scientific species names. Their catch is logged as a specimen variant attached to the base species ID.

| ABR-002 | Bentspine Flathead | Bartail flathead, DP / SH | Curved spine, uneven posture, asymmetric swimming | Lies in sand but has difficulty making a fast ambush; hides sooner when disturbed | Bottom bait; lower condition grade; automatic research flag if severe deformity |

| ABR-005 | Cloud-eye Snapper | Russell's snapper or John's snapper, SH / DP | One eye has an opaque or scarred surface | Relies more on lateral-line cues and may startle at close approach; avoids exposed water | Rod / bait; inspection required before any food sale |

### 3.1 Aberrant specimen rules

- A visually unusual animal is not automatically supernatural.
- Pigment variation alone does not mean the animal is sick or unsafe; require inspection only where the game gives another reason.
- Severe deformity, parasite burden, unexplained lesions, or abnormal internal tissue must be held for research rather than sold as food.
- Injury variants should be rare individuals, not a region-wide population unless a quest explicitly investigates a broader pattern.
- Do not tell the player that pollution caused a deformity without evidence. The research NPC may present multiple hypotheses and uncertainty.
- A specimen's abnormal appearance does not make it more nutritious, more powerful, or inherently more valuable.
- If released, the specimen should resume its ordinary survival behavior where feasible; it should not vanish merely because the player chose not to sell it.

### 3.2 Aberrant specimen trade

| Specimen condition | Buyer / outcome | Payment |
|---|---|---|
| Harmless pigment variant, inspected | Ordinary fish buyer or collector | Normal or modest collector premium |
| Healed injury with good condition | Ordinary buyer after inspection | Normal or discounted grade |
| Severe deformity | Research station | One-time research contract |
| Heavy parasite burden or unexplained lesions | Quarantine / research station | Small sample bounty; not a food sale |
| Protected species with an abnormal trait | Release / conservation report | Research or conservation reward only |

Do not implement a universal "mutant multiplier." Value comes from an actual buyer's request and specimen condition, not from the word "mutated."

Exact base-species rates, inspection discounts, first-record research rewards, and the one-time contract payouts for UNC specimens are defined in the [Seafood Selling Price Schedule](../07_GAME_RULES_AND_BALANCING/SEAFOOD-SELLING-PRICE-SCHEDULE.md). That schedule is authoritative; do not duplicate or independently rebalance prices in this document.

## 4. Unnatural catchables — impossible specimens

These entities are physically catchable under specific conditions but are not food animals. They are minor discovery encounters, not additional major bosses. Their unnatural rule must be observable before capture.

| ID | Name | Location | Unnatural rule | Encounter / catch condition | Specimen outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UNC-001 | The Tideglass Mullet | Greyhaven outer estuary | Its flank reflects a tide line that belongs to a different water level; the reflection changes before the real tide | Appears only near slack water; compare a visual reading with a Rig pulse, then bait the location shown by the second return | Sealed research specimen; not edible |
| UNC-002 | The Bellbone Croaker | Drowned Parish bell tower | Its body cavity produces a bell-like interval even when no sound source is struck | Stop engine and horn; use one short pulse after the false bell; catch with a quiet bottom line in the tower's outer street | Acoustic research sample; no food market |
| UNC-003 | The Backstep Eel | Blue Maw and flooded culverts | It travels through a passage while its wake and sediment trail move in the opposite direction | Map its loop from a fixed landmark; place a baited eel pot at the exit it should reach, not the point where its echo appears | Contained sample or quest specimen; cannot be farmed |
| UNC-004 | The Seamfin Shoal | Outer Shoals / Widow's Teeth | The school maintains perfect spacing while passing through currents that should disperse it; individual fish briefly occupy the same apparent position | Observe from outside the school; wait for its formation to split; jig only the isolated return | One or two research specimens per event; school withdraws after capture |
| UNC-005 | The Lanternless Lure | Lantern Drift | A luminous lure remains visible after its body has disappeared from the Rig and camera view; it responds to pulses as if it were bait | Do not chase the light directly; follow the real vertical migration layer and lower a deep jig beneath the false lure during its dark interval | Lure-like tissue sample; not edible and not repeatable in the same migration event |
| UNC-006 | The Saltless Eel | Blue Maw / Drowned Parish salinity boundary | It crosses a sharp salinity boundary without changing behavior, then appears in a separate water mass without traversing the channel | Track the same acoustic signature across two salinity readings; use a baited pot at the only physically connected passage | Quarantined specimen; research contract only |
| UNC-007 | The Hollowjaw Grenadier | Siltfall Scar | Its stomach return contains an acoustic pattern recorded by the player's Rig several minutes after the fish was caught | A low-strength deep-line signal reveals the pattern; retrieve slowly and do not trigger a second pulse during the fight | Specimen is logged with its impossible recording; no edible sale |
| UNC-008 | The Unmoored Fry | Any region after a major anomaly event; rare | A tiny juvenile appears in a sealed catch container despite no fish entering the net, trap, or hook | Triggered only after the player records a qualifying anomaly; inspect the empty gear before opening the container | One-time quest specimen; releasing it is a valid outcome; never ordinary food |

### 4.1 Unnatural specimen capture principles

- Capturing the specimen must require observation and a learnable rule, not a hidden percentage alone.
- The catch condition should use the Sounding Rig, tide, current, depth, bait, location, or an event clue in a meaningful way.
- A failed attempt may consume bait or time, but must not arbitrarily destroy high-value gear.
- No unnatural specimen should create an unlimited money farm.
- A caught specimen may remain alive, become inert, disappear, or leave only a trace according to its authored rule. Never apply one universal supernatural explanation.
- If a specimen cannot be physically retained under its own rule, reward the player with an acoustic record, photograph, residue, or research entry rather than pretending it fits in a normal fish crate.

## 5. Specimen journal and identification

Each catchable abnormality uses a three-stage discovery process:

1. **Unclassified:** Player records visual / acoustic evidence. The journal lists an ordinary base species if identifiable, plus "unusual specimen."
2. **Compared:** Player returns to a known habitat, compares another specimen or repeats a non-invasive observation. The journal records a tentative explanation.
3. **Reviewed:** A qualified NPC or research station issues a classification: harmless variation, injury, parasite / disease concern, or anomalous. The player is told what is known and what remains uncertain.

The journal must distinguish:
- Confirmed observation.
- Plausible biological explanation.
- Unverified local rumor.
- Impossible observation / anomaly rule.

Do not make the player diagnose an infectious disease from a single visual cue.

## 6. New buyer and reward category

Add a **Specimen Research Desk** at the research station or a later-game contact in Greyhaven. It is not a second fishmonger.

It accepts:
- First verified record of an aberrant morph.
- Carefully preserved tissue or scale sample where allowed by the quest.
- Acoustic signatures and visual survey data.
- One-time capture of an unnatural specimen.
- Successful release and documentation of a protected unusual animal.

It rejects:
- Repeated specimens with no new information.
- Unsafe or unverified food products.
- Protected wildlife offered for sale.
- Ordinary fish already covered by normal market orders.

Research rewards can include currency, equipment blueprints, Sounding Rig calibration, map annotations, access to a new location, or NPC trust. They should be finite contract rewards, not a repeatable premium price for every catch.

## 7. Integration with ecology and AI

- Aberrant specimens inherit the base species' ordinary habitat, diet, migration, and life-cycle behavior unless the specific condition changes it.
- An injured animal should shelter, reduce activity, or avoid predators more often; it does not automatically become aggressive.
- Parasite-marked specimens can be visually noticed by predators or separated from a school, but do not assume every parasite changes behavior.
- Unnatural catchables use the existing life / tactical / anomaly architecture. The anomaly layer supplies the impossible rule; the life layer still handles movement, energy, feeding, and rest where applicable.
- An anomaly must not overwrite the base fish stock or create population without an authored event.
- Capture and release are persistent events when they affect a quest or named specimen.
- The Threat Director controls encounter opportunity and cooldown; do not add independent random encounter rolls in each catchable.

## 8. Data fields

Add optional fields to the species / catch data model:

- specimenClass: ordinary, aberrant, unnatural, protected.
- baseSpeciesId: ordinary species ID when the catch is a variant.
- aberrationTags: pigment, injury, skeletal, ocular, parasite, developmental, unknown.
- anomalyRuleId: stable reference to the unnatural rule, if applicable.
- researchOnly: whether the specimen cannot enter ordinary food markets.
- inspectionRequired: whether a buyer must clear the specimen.
- retentionPolicy: retain, release, sample-only, quest-controlled.
- eventLimit: maximum valid recovery per authored anomaly event.
- researchRewardId: finite contract reward reference.

Do not put these values into a shared base-species ScriptableObject if they vary per individual specimen. Use a catch-instance record or a linked specimen-variant definition.

## 9. Acceptance tests

- The normal 100 fish and 47 invertebrate totals remain unchanged.
- Aberrant specimens are variants of base species, not duplicate scientific taxa.
- A harmless pigment variant is not automatically classified as diseased.
- Parasite / lesion / severe deformity cases are not sold as food without inspection.
- Unnatural specimens have one observable rule and one learnable capture condition each.
- Every unnatural specimen has a finite encounter / research reward rule.
- Protected wildlife remains protected regardless of unusual appearance.
- A specimen's capture, release, or sampling result persists correctly when it is quest-critical.
- No anomaly can be caught repeatedly for unlimited money.
- The journal labels evidence and hypotheses separately.
