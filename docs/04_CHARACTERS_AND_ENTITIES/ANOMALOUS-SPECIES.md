# Anomalous Species and Site-Specific Entities

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed creature roster — no unapproved entity is final canon  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.1  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-10  
> Scope: Unnatural entities, unusual encounter presences, site-specific triggers, evidence, limits, and counterplay

## 1. Design contract

Anomalous entities are not ordinary wildlife with extra health. They are identifiable violations of the world's ecological or physical expectations. Each one needs:

1. A normal-looking habitat and a reason it is associated with that place.
2. A baseline behavior that the player can observe before an encounter.
3. A specific anomaly: what rule is being broken?
4. A trigger that can be learned.
5. Evidence or warning signals before escalation.
6. Limits and counterplay.
7. A reason it does not attack constantly.
8. A lifecycle or persistence rule so it feels like an inhabitant of the world, not a random event.

Keep the ordinary animal ecology in REGIONAL-ECOLOGY-AND-CREATURES.md and the general life-state architecture in CREATURE-LIFE-CYCLES-AND-ECOLOGY.md. The anomalous controller must not replace feeding, sheltering, resting, injury, migration, or other underlying life states.

**Content budget:** The project has a planning target of 6–8 major threat entities. The following roster contains six major threat/presence designs and two environmental or colony-scale anomalies. Several are not combat encounters. Do not turn every location into a boss arena.

## 2. Anomaly classification

| Class | Purpose | Player danger |
|---|---|---|
| Observer | Establishes presence, watches, follows at a distance | Low / indirect |
| Territorial ambusher | Defends a narrow habitat or shelter | High within a defined zone |
| Acoustic mimic | Uses or corrupts familiar sounds | Variable |
| Migratory stalker | Tracks movement or disturbance over a long route | Escalating, not constant |
| Deep-water hunter | Full hostile predator encounter | High |
| Colony / environmental anomaly | Alters local signals, ecology, or perception | Usually indirect |

## 3. Regional entities

## 3.1 The Mooring Guest

**Region:** Greyhaven outer harbor, old breakwater, and the approach to the harbor mouth.  
**Class:** Observer; non-combat.  
**Biological silhouette:** A large, ray-like outline that rests near the bottom beneath the older pilings. It should remain ambiguous whether it is an animal, a shadow, or an acoustic return.

**Baseline**
- It remains beneath a particular stretch of old pier or breakwater.
- It is visible only under certain light angles, water clarity, and tide conditions.
- Small fish may pass over it without scattering.
- It does not attack the player or enter the central safe harbor.

**Anomalous rule**
Its position appears stable from shore but changes in Sounding Rig returns without a corresponding movement path. If the player marks its location repeatedly, the same return can appear on more than one nearby piling line.

**Triggers**
- Low-noise observation at slack water may reveal it.
- Repeated Sounding Rig pulses produce a second, delayed return.
- Direct approach causes it to vanish from sight without a visible departure.

**Evidence**
- A broad bottom return with no clear body contour.
- Small fish briefly swim around an empty patch of water.
- A wet, low-frequency vibration through the pier supports, without an obvious wave impact.

**Limits and counterplay**
- It is never a combat threat.
- It cannot follow the boat out of the harbor.
- Waiting for a different tide or viewing angle produces a different observation.
- The player should learn that not every anomaly needs to be confronted.

**Ecological role**
This entity is an observation mystery, not a predator. It exists to establish the idea that the sea can be present and responsive without becoming hostile.

## 3.2 The Glassback

**Region:** The Shallows, particularly seagrass edges, sand channels, and patch-reef transitions.  
**Class:** Ambush predator; conditional threat.  
**Biological silhouette:** A broad, low-bodied predator with reflective, irregular plates that resemble broken shell, bleached coral, or sunlit sand. Its shape is difficult to read from above.

**Baseline**
- It hunts small fish and crustaceans in the habitat, not boats.
- It remains close to the seabed and uses seagrass or reef shadow for cover.
- It may wait motionless for long periods.
- It abandons a hunting site after prey density falls or repeated disturbance makes feeding inefficient.

**Anomalous rule**
Its reflected pattern occasionally shows the seagrass moving in a direction opposite to the actual current. The distortion is visible only at certain angles and does not occur on every encounter.

**Triggers**
- Repeated Sounding Rig pulses over one feeding patch.
- A boat repeatedly crossing its ambush lane.
- Injuring it or blocking its retreat route.
- Fishing pressure that removes the small prey it normally hunts.

**Escalation**
1. Prey schools leave the area.
2. A reflective patch appears where no exposed sand or shell should be.
3. The creature changes position while the player's view is occluded by surface glare or vegetation.
4. It makes a short approach toward the boat's shadow.
5. If the player continues to intrude, it makes one telegraphed strike or hull-side impact, then retreats.

**Limits and counterplay**
- It has a small territory; it cannot pursue the player across the whole region.
- It cannot see clearly through dense suspended sediment.
- It avoids open, brightly illuminated sand where it has no cover.
- Leaving its hunting lane and allowing time for prey to return prevents repeated escalation.

**Ecological role**
It is a low-frequency ambush predator. Most of the time it hunts ordinary prey and is invisible to the player. The rare boat encounter is caused by disturbance of its feeding territory, not an arbitrary hunt order.

## 3.3 The Cliffblind

**Region:** Blackrock Reach, The Undercliff, and selected basalt cavities.  
**Class:** Territorial ambusher; major threat candidate.  
**Biological silhouette:** A heavy, flattened animal-like form integrated with fractured basalt. The visible outline should resemble a collapsed rock shelf until it moves.

**Baseline**
- It occupies one or more deep crevices.
- It feeds on cave fish, crustaceans, and animals forced through surge channels.
- It remains still during strong wave surge and uses calmer intervals to reposition.
- It returns to the same shelter after feeding.

**Anomalous rule**
The Cliffblind can produce a convincing duplicate of a nearby rock contour in Sounding Rig data. Its acoustic return appears attached to the seafloor even when its physical position has changed.

**Triggers**
- Entering its den chamber.
- Repeated acoustic pulses at the chamber entrance.
- Blocking the only safe retreat crack.
- Remaining inside its short territorial range after warning cues.

**Warning signals**
- Crustaceans retreat deep into cracks.
- Cave fish stop crossing a particular opening.
- A low resonance repeats at the interval of the local wave surge, then fails to match it.
- The Sounding Rig shows a rock contour that changes only after the player moves.

**Attack logic**
- One short lunge from cover.
- A missed lunge creates a recovery window.
- It does not pursue into open water.
- It returns to its shelter when the player exits the defended zone.

**Limits and counterplay**
- Cannot leave its basalt habitat.
- Cannot sustain a long open-water chase.
- Strong surge and deep cracks make its movement predictable but dangerous.
- The player can retreat through the wider passage, stop pulsing the Rig, and wait for it to settle.

**Ecological role**
Its natural prey keeps it near cave systems. It is territorial, not a roaming sea monster.

## 3.4 The Bell Eel

**Region:** The Drowned Parish, flooded bell tower, submerged streets, and connected culverts.  
**Class:** Acoustic mimic; stalk / strike encounter candidate.  
**Biological silhouette:** A long, ribbon-like form seen only in fragmented glimpses through doorways, broken windows, and silt.

**Baseline**
- It occupies a network of submerged masonry passages.
- It feeds on fish and crustaceans sheltering among the old buildings.
- It retreats through narrow cracks and avoids broad, exposed water.
- It follows low-current routes between feeding spaces.

**Anomalous rule**
It reproduces the timing and decay of the parish bell, even when the bell is physically damaged or absent. Its echo seems to originate from a different building than the one containing the source.

**Triggers**
- Repeated Sounding Rig pulses near the bell tower.
- Striking metal wreckage or using a horn in the flooded streets.
- Following the false bell sound into a confined passage.
- Remaining near its feeding route after the warning pattern.

**Warning signals**
- Small fish leave the bell tower before the sound begins.
- The first echo is physically plausible; the second appears to arrive from an impossible direction.
- The local current does not change when the sound repeats.
- A ribbon-shaped shadow passes behind an opening rather than across it.

**Attack logic**
- It investigates the last strong sound source.
- It circles through building cavities instead of tracking the boat's live position perfectly.
- It may strike the hull from a blind side only after a clear warning interval.
- One strike is followed by withdrawal into the structure.

**Limits and counterplay**
- Cannot pursue beyond the submerged settlement's connected structure network.
- Does not attack in open, current-swept water.
- The player can stop producing repeated sounds, leave the tower's sound corridor, and use the Sounding Rig from a different angle to identify the false echo.

**Ecological role**
It is tied to the flooded architecture and its acoustic geometry. It is not an ordinary eel and should not be presented as a real animal.

## 3.5 The Countercurrent

**Region:** Outer Shoals, Widow's Teeth, Outer Wall, and migratory current corridors.  
**Class:** Migratory stalker; large-scale encounter candidate.  
**Biological silhouette:** A vast shape below schools of fish, usually perceived through displaced water, a moving dark band, or an incomplete Rig trace rather than a fully visible model.

**Baseline**
- It follows a recurring current corridor.
- It appears around prey concentrations and upwelling edges.
- It does not stay at one location.
- Smaller animals may alter depth or scatter as it passes.
- It can ignore the player completely.

**Anomalous rule**
Its apparent travel direction contradicts the local current and known route. It can appear at two distant sounding points without enough time to travel between them.

**Triggers**
- Repeatedly following it across multiple current corridors.
- Repeated high-energy Rig pulses during a pass.
- Approaching a dense feeding aggregation while its route is active.
- Returning to a recently abandoned route before its passage has ended.

**Escalation**
1. Surface birds stop diving and climb or disperse.
2. A baitfish school compresses into a narrow band.
3. A large return appears below the school.
4. The return moves across the current rather than with it.
5. The player receives a distant pass or wake disturbance.
6. Only after repeated disturbance can the entity begin a short stalking sequence.

**Limits and counterplay**
- It is not a permanent boss with a fixed spawn point.
- It cannot turn instantly or follow the player through shallow reef passages.
- It does not attack from beneath safe or sheltered water.
- The player can break pursuit, reduce noise, change course, and allow the migration corridor to clear.

**Ecological role**
It resembles a large migratory predator in the way it follows food concentrations, but its impossible route behavior is the supernatural element. Most sightings should remain distant.

## 3.6 The Sleeper

**Region:** Hollow Trench, deep slope, and its established quest encounter corridor.  
**Class:** Principal hostile deep-water predator.  
**Status:** Existing encounter design; retain its current state machine and numeric tuning unless a separate approved change is made.

**Ecological baseline**
- Solitary deep-water ambush predator.
- Remains inactive or shelters in deep terrain between feeding opportunities.
- Hunts suitable prey using low movement, acoustic awareness, and a short ambush window.
- Recovers after exertion and withdraws rather than chasing indefinitely.
- May investigate a strong sound source before deciding whether to escalate toward the boat.

**Anomalous rule**
It appears to retain or relocate acoustic information in ways inconsistent with ordinary sensory memory and distance. Its approach can make successive returns appear closer than its visible movement should allow.

**Encounter contract**
- The existing quest and threat director own activation gates.
- It must warn before a strike.
- It investigates the last meaningful sound source rather than receiving perfect live player coordinates.
- Its pursuit is bounded.
- It has a recover and disengage phase.
- It must not one-hit kill the boat.
- Counterplay remains based on noise discipline, heading changes, route choice, occlusion, and shallow exclusion areas.

## 4. Environmental and colony-scale anomalies

These are not conventional enemies. They create an encounter, affect wildlife, and make the environment feel alive without requiring combat.

## 4.1 The Marrow Choir

**Location:** The Bone Orchard.  
**Class:** Colony-scale acoustic anomaly; non-combat.

The whale-fall community is ordinary at first: mobile scavengers arrive, soft tissue is consumed, enrichment opportunists colonize the remains, and later bone-associated organisms and microbial communities use the remaining nutrients.

The anomaly begins when the exposed skeleton produces a series of low resonances that appear synchronized across separated bones. The resonance is not an actual voice. It may be caused by an unknown colony-like presence occupying the skeleton, but the game should not answer this immediately.

**Behavior**
- Quiet when scavengers are feeding.
- Stronger during still-water periods or when nearby animals leave.
- Changes pattern after a Sounding Rig pulse.
- Can attract or displace nearby scavengers, altering the natural food-fall behavior.

**Player interaction**
- Observe the natural succession before disturbing the site.
- Compare the skeleton's returns at different angles.
- Stop pulsing and wait for the echo to decay.
- Do not turn it into a mandatory combat encounter.

## 4.2 The Borrowed Light

**Location:** Lantern Drift and nearby midwater layers.  
**Class:** Visual / acoustic mimic; non-combat or indirect threat.

The natural baseline is diel vertical migration: many midwater animals rise toward shallower waters at night and descend toward deeper waters during daylight. Lanternfish, bristlemouths, squid, and gelatinous animals provide a moving food layer for larger predators.

The anomaly creates a small pattern of lights that resembles scattered bioluminescent organisms but maintains spacing and rhythm inconsistent with a natural school.

**Behavior**
- Appears near the natural migration layer, not on a fixed coordinate.
- Can be confused with ordinary bioluminescence at first.
- Changes its rhythm after a Rig pulse.
- May cause small fish to aggregate or scatter in a way that draws larger predators.

**Player interaction**
- Compare the light pattern to nearby school movement.
- Use a short pulse rather than repeated high-power pings.
- Observe whether the apparent school rises or descends with the real migration layer.
- A player who follows it blindly can enter a dangerous depth band, but the event must provide readable warnings.

## 5. Encounter frequency and fairness

Anomalies should be rare compared with ordinary wildlife activity.

- Most wildlife sightings are non-hostile.
- Most anomalous observations end without a chase.
- Territorial threats remain tied to their habitat.
- Large migratory entities are usually distant.
- Colony-scale anomalies alter the environment rather than attacking.
- A hostile encounter should require a valid trigger, warning, and escalation window.
- Safe zones, cutscenes, and protected quest states block hostile activation.
- Repeated identical triggers should be controlled by cooldowns and deterministic event IDs.

The Threat Director and probability documents remain the source of encounter scheduling. Do not add independent random rolls inside every creature behavior.

## 6. Required data for every new entity

Each future anomalous entity needs:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Entity ID | Stable unique key |
| Region / sublocation | Exact habitat association |
| Class | Observer, territorial, mimic, migratory, hunter, or colony |
| Baseline ecology | Food, shelter, active period, and non-hostile behavior |
| Anomalous rule | The precise physical or ecological rule it violates |
| State model | Dormant, investigate, warn, stalk, attack, withdraw, recover |
| Perception | Visual, acoustic, vibration, chemical cue, or supernatural channel |
| Trigger | Valid conditions and hard exclusions |
| Telegraph | Observable warning before escalation |
| Movement envelope | Speed, turn, depth, range, and navigation limits |
| Attack contract | Damage, telegraph, recovery, cooldown, and target rules if hostile |
| Counterplay | Learnable ways to disengage or survive |
| Persistence | What is saved and what resets |
| Audio signature | Distinct signal that can be captioned or represented visually |
| Tests | Trigger, warning, fairness, limits, save/load, and edge cases |
