# Blackrock Reach — Exposed Basalt Coast

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed regional design — not a connected playable destination yet  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-14

## Regional identity

Blackrock Reach is the exposed side of the island chain: steep volcanic headlands, dark rock shelves, surge channels, undercut cliffs and narrow water between offshore stacks. It is not simply a darker version of the Shallows. Its defining pressures are wave energy, changing wind, reflected sound and the difficulty of judging distance against a broken coastline.

The coast is geologically dramatic but not inherently supernatural. Basalt faces, cavities and submerged ledges can create confusing echoes and dangerous approach angles without an intelligent source.

## Landmarks and places

- **The Split Face:** a tall basalt wall divided by an old fracture; a strong visual landmark from safe water.
- **The Needle Stack:** a narrow offshore rock pillar that can be hidden by spray or fog.
- **The Backwash Cut:** a narrow channel where reflected swell and cross-current complicate steering.
- **The Glass Cave:** a sea cave with a bright entrance and a dark interior; the player should not be required to enter it by boat.
- **The Lee Shelf:** a temporary sheltered approach behind a headland, safe only under suitable conditions.

All place names are proposed. Exact geometry, approach depths and route lengths must be tested before these are charted.

## Ecology

The exposed coast supports attached organisms, crevice-dwelling fish, crustaceans, seabirds, schooling fish and predators that use current edges. Wildlife should exploit shelter and surge rather than patrol the player as a default enemy.

The region may support a distinctive resident species or rare encounter, but its behaviour must be designed from habitat and prey. Do not make a large animal the explanation for every damaged hull.

## Narrative use

Blackrock Reach is the first major test of ordinary explanations. The player may hear an apparent second return from a wall, cave or submerged rock face. By changing heading, distance and engine state, they can identify which echoes are repeatable and which remain inconsistent.

A later signal may resemble the Orison's delayed-return pattern, but it should not be an exact copy of the first incident. The player needs to compare evidence across locations rather than receive a scripted answer from the cliffs.

## Sound and visual identity

- Hard contrast between dark basalt and pale foam.
- Strong surface breakup near exposed rock; smoother water in a temporary lee.
- Wind against cliff edges, surf, loose stones and seabird calls.
- Echoes that change with position and heading.
- Rare periods of reduced ambient sound, carefully separated from normal lulls.

Avoid permanent black fog, glowing runes, giant skull rock formations or other shorthand that turns the coast into an obvious haunted attraction.

## Hazards and counterplay

- Breaking swell and surge near rock faces.
- Narrow channels and submerged ledges.
- Poor visibility from spray and fog.
- Echo confusion around caves and cliffs.

Provide readable water, warning context and a viable retreat route. The player should be able to retreat to the lee shelf or open water when conditions allow.

## Story role

Blackrock Reach belongs to Act II. It demonstrates that some strange returns have physical explanations, while a smaller subset remains difficult to reconcile. It should not reveal the Choir's origin or the full Orison story.

## Acceptance questions

- Does the region feel exposed and navigationally different from the Shallows?
- Can the player verify at least one ordinary acoustic reflection?
- Is the uncertain return clearly distinct from the natural echoes?
- Does the player have fair warning and a route to disengage?
