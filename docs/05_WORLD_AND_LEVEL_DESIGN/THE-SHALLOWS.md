# The Shallows — Reef Flat and Seagrass Shelf

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed regional design — ecology baseline exists; final level and travel implementation pending  
> Owner: Project lead

> Last reviewed: 2026-11-14

## Regional identity

The Shallows are the first open-water grounds beyond Greyhaven's sheltered bay. They are a broad, relatively bright shelf of seagrass, patch reefs, sandy gutters, coral heads and shallow channels. From the boat, the seabed can appear close enough to read, but the water is not uniformly safe: tide, reef clearance, weather and changing sand can make a familiar passage unreliable.

The region should feel alive before it feels mysterious. Grazing fish, turtles, small reef fish, crustaceans, octopuses, rays and passing predators create movement and sound at several scales. The Ribbon Grazer is a proposed game-native herbivore and a visual sign of a healthy meadow, not a threat.

## Landmarks and places

- **The Glass Channel:** a clear sand gutter between reef patches; a baseline location for comparing depth and bottom visibility.
- **Ribbon Meadow:** a seagrass bed where grazing groups pass through and juvenile fish shelter.
- **The Split Reef:** two reef heads with a navigable cut between them; the player must read tide and clearance.
- **The Old Marker Line:** weathered navigational stakes or buoys whose recorded positions require periodic correction.
- **The Outer Shelf Edge:** where the shallow bottom begins to fall away toward deeper water.

These are proposed place names, not currently verified scene objects or chart discoveries.

## Ecology

Use the regional ecology specification as the detailed habitat reference. Keep ordinary fish behaviour driven by feeding, cover, time, disturbance and migration. Do not make the Reef, Meadow or Marker Line a permanent monster arena.

Fishing pressure can reduce local availability and should recover through existing or planned ecology rules. Catching a predator should not make the entire region magically empty; it changes a local food web only where the shared simulation supports that behaviour.

## Narrative use

1. Teach the player what an ordinary sounding return looks like.
2. Establish biological scattering layers and natural sound sources.
3. Provide a low-risk place to test a suspicious reading against a known bottom and visible landmark.
4. Introduce one ambiguous sound that can be explained by wildlife, reef geometry or equipment.
5. Let the player leave without being punished for not accepting a supernatural interpretation.

The Shallows should not reveal the Orison truth. They teach the player to build a baseline and distinguish "unusual" from "impossible".

## Sound and visual identity

- Sunlit water, moving caustics where affordable, visible bottom changes, reef shadow and shifting surface texture.
- Small, distinct calls and movement from wildlife rather than a constant ominous drone.
- Natural changes in visibility and sound caused by current, depth and weather.
- A first anomalous cue should be subtle and testable; no immediate chase or forced damage.

## Hazards and counterplay

The primary hazards are shallow clearance, reef contact, weather exposure and route mistakes. Warning and counterplay must be fair. The region should retain a clear return direction based on known coastal landmarks once those are implemented; do not add GPS or a hidden compass before the chart system supports it.

## Acceptance questions

- Can the player recognise at least one ordinary depth / habitat baseline?
- Can the player tell a natural acoustic event from a repeated sounding return?
- Does the region offer useful fishing and survey work without constant threat escalation?
- Are its named places clearly distinguished from discovered map marks?
