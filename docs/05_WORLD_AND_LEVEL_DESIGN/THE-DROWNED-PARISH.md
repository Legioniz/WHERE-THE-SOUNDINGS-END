# The Drowned Parish — The Lower Settlement

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed regional history and level design — submerged remains are a campaign target, not a playable level yet  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-14

## Regional identity

The Drowned Parish is the submerged footprint of an older coastal district near the former inlet. Its remains include low walls, foundations, storage spaces, fragments of a landing structure and an older route that once connected the lower settlement to higher ground.

The region should feel like a place where people worked and lived, not an underwater dungeon built for treasure. Household materials, repaired fittings, work tools and public records matter more than piles of gold or a collection of monster trophies.

The name is a later mariner's label for the remains. It does not establish one catastrophic flood, a universal local religion or a supernatural cause.

## Historical model

The lower settlement became progressively difficult to occupy because of changing channels, repeated storm damage, shoreline movement and possible local ground change. Families relocated in stages. Some structures were dismantled; others were abandoned or reused as salvage. The exact chronology and physical cause remain open for research and project-lead approval.

A bell structure appears in the region's oral history and may survive among the remains. It is not automatically the Greyhaven lighthouse bell. Reports of a bell beneath the water must be treated as witness testimony until the player finds material evidence.

## Landmarks and places

- **The Old Landing:** remnants of a lower-water loading point and mooring structures.
- **The Bell House:** a proposed communal structure associated with local notices and gathering; its original use and surviving fittings require evidence.
- **The Sunken Lane:** a route between foundations, partly concealed by sediment and marine growth.
- **The Record Room:** a proposed storage space where fragments of dated records may survive; it must not contain a complete exposition dump.
- **The Upper Steps:** remains of a path leading toward the higher settlement.

These names are design labels. They do not imply that each location already exists in Unity or that the player can safely navigate through it.

## Environmental storytelling

Tell the history through:
- foundations that stop at an old waterline;
- salvaged timber reused in later harbour buildings;
- household objects left in place or removed carefully;
- different generations of repair on the same structure;
- records with original entries and later annotations;
- a gap where a bell mount or public fitting used to be.

Avoid intact, perfectly preserved streets or a single room containing every answer. Water, sediment, biological growth and later salvage should have changed the place.

## Narrative use

The Drowned Parish belongs to Act III. Its records establish that reports of unusual soundings and bell-like events predate the Orison. The player then compares the older records with the Orison route fragment and Nell's radio evidence.

The Parish should change the protagonist's understanding of the past but not reveal the Choir's ultimate origin. It should clarify what the Orison crew tried to do and leave open why the signal behaved as it did.

## Ecology and hazards

The remains support attached organisms, sheltering fish, crustaceans, scavengers and sediment-dwelling life. Flooded structures can create entanglement, unstable clearance and poor visibility. Wildlife remains ecological, not automatically hostile.

Any diving, pressure, current or entanglement hazard requires its own equipment, warning and counterplay design. The current boat prototype does not imply a finished underwater exploration controller.

## Evidence rules

- Distinguish original records from copied or later retold accounts.
- Establish whether an object was found in situ or moved by salvage.
- Do not identify a speaker by a bell sound alone.
- Keep the Parish bell and lighthouse bell histories separate.
- Provide more than one path to the core pre-Orison chronology.

## Acceptance questions

- Can players understand that this was once an ordinary inhabited place?
- Do the remains show gradual abandonment and reuse rather than one unexplained catastrophe?
- Does the evidence predate the Orison using a credible date source?
- Does the region preserve uncertainty instead of solving the entire mystery?
