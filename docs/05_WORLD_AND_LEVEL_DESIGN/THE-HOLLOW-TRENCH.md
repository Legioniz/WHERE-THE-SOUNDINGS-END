# The Hollow Trench — The Last Reliable Sounding

> Project: WHERE THE SOUNDINGS END  
> Status: Proposed endgame region — isolated survey sandbox exists; campaign travel and final story are not implemented  
> Owner: Project lead  
> Version: 0.2  
> Last reviewed: 2026-11-14

## Regional identity

The Hollow Trench is the deepest planned region and the edge of the protagonist's reliable survey knowledge. Its upper approaches include steep slope, submarine canyon margins, hardground, seep features and isolated deep-water habitat. The final narrative boundary is not simply the greatest depth on the chart; it is the place where repeated measurements can no longer establish a dependable bottom.

The region should feel immense and quiet without becoming a featureless black void. Deep-water life remains present: scavengers, slow-moving animals, microbial communities, seep organisms and large mobile species that use the slope or canyon as habitat.

## Physical and ecological identity

Existing survey-sandbox content includes authored brine-pool, cold-seep, hydrate and whale-fall evidence sites. These are research/inspection prototypes, not completed hazard simulations or proof that the entire region has been modelled.

Keep physical science and fictional anomaly separate:
- a brine pool has distinct density and visibility properties;
- a cold seep supports chemically driven communities;
- gas hydrate structures belong to specific pressure / temperature conditions;
- a whale fall supports a changing sequence of scavengers and decomposers;
- the Choir remains an unresolved acoustic mystery.

Do not make every deep-water habitat a source of hostile creatures or unexplained signals.

## Landmarks and places

- **The Upper Slope:** transition from offshore ridge and canyon margin into deeper water.
- **The Seep Field:** a localised zone of chemical discharge and distinctive benthic life.
- **The Brine Boundary:** a dense, visually distinct water mass in a depression.
- **The Fall Site:** a whale-fall evidence location that shows deep-sea succession.
- **The Last Reliable Return:** a narrative designation for the outer edge of dependable sounding evidence.

The names are proposed content labels. Exact bathymetry, coordinates, depth values and travel routes must come from technical/world-map data, not prose.

## Narrative use

The Hollow Trench belongs to Acts III and IV. It contains the strongest convergence of Orison route evidence and the final acoustic boundary. The player should arrive after a sequence of increasingly difficult but testable survey decisions.

At the boundary:
- the first return may be plausible;
- a later return may contradict the first;
- repeated checks may reduce confidence rather than create certainty;
- visual landmarks and physical notes may remain useful even when the Sounding Rig cannot provide a reliable bottom;
- a familiar voice may be heard but must not provide new objective information.

The final choice is whether to follow the voice, stop answering or mark the position and preserve the evidence.

## Sound and visual identity

- Vast water columns with small, readable sources of scale: particles, lights, a distant ridge, the boat's own wake and the survey instrument.
- Restrained low-frequency movement, distant biological sound and long intervals without score.
- Visual contrasts from particles, sediment, chemical plumes and occasional living light.
- No permanent ghost-blue palette, glowing runes or obvious monster silhouette at the final boundary.

Silence should feel like the removal of an established soundscape, not a constant background effect.

## Hazards and counterplay

Potential hazards include pressure-rated equipment limits, current edges, entanglement, poor visibility, vessel range and the inability to verify the bottom. These hazards require specific equipment and system designs before implementation. The current survey sandbox does not establish full pressure, heat, chemistry, entanglement or deep-water navigation mechanics.

The player must understand the preparation cost and retain the option to turn back before the final boundary.

## Acceptance questions

- Is the deep ocean portrayed as an ecosystem rather than a monster waiting room?
- Are brine pools, seeps, hydrates and whale falls distinguished from fictional anomalies?
- Does the final boundary challenge the player's confidence in evidence without secretly disabling controls?
- Does the final action remain a clear player decision?
